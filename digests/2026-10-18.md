### 1. [Frontier Pre-training at Microsoft AI (MAI-Base-2)](https://microsoft.ai/news/frontier-pre-training-at-microsoft-ai/)

Microsoft AI blog · news · scaling

**In one line:** Microsoft AI details MAI-Base-2: 2.8T-total/80B-active MoE base model, 50T tokens on 16k GB200s, Muon optimizer

**What:** Pre-training report (dated Oct 15) for MAI-Base-2, a 2.8T-total / 80B-active MoE base model with native encoder-free vision; post-training is still in progress. Changes vs MAI-Base-1: MoE in every layer (MAI-Base-1 alternated dense FFN and MoE), made affordable by a multi-round dropless MoE with fixed-size memory that raised MoE-layer MFU from 24% to 35% (grouped GEMM/communication, GPU-initiated all-to-all, symmetric-memory token exchange). AdamW replaced by Muon (2.1% of iteration time). Activation-gradient clipping at chosen locations instead of only global clipping. Published hyperparameter scaling laws for LR, LR×WD and dropout, derived from a fixed-recipe scaling ladder with tokens-per-active-parameter held constant. Trained on a 50T-token mixture on 16k GB200 GPUs for 38 days at 21% average MFU and 89% goodput. Weights go to trusted partners through Foundry, not as open weights.

**Result:** Microsoft claims 4.2–12.3x better pre-training FLOPs efficiency than DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base and 6.3–12.7x over MAI-Base-1, and lower held-out bits-per-byte than every open-weight model it tested (DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base, Kimi K3, Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B, MiMo-V2.6-Pro-RL).

**Critique:** The efficiency multiples are compute-matching estimates from Microsoft's own fitted scaling laws, not head-to-head training runs. Three of the four comparison models were evaluated only as post-trained checkpoints, which the post itself says may affect the BPB comparison. No downstream benchmarks yet, the weights are not public, and the forward-pass weight quantization format is not specified, so none of this can be checked independently. 21% MFU is modest for a run of this size.

### 3. [Joint Subspace Sparse Attention for Efficient Long-Context Decoding](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19026)

HF Daily Papers · paper · inference

**In one line:** JSSA: training-free token-level sparse attention with a gather-free GQA kernel; up to 6.43x decode throughput at 128K on B200

**What:** Token-level sparse attention for decoding. Instead of scoring every cached key, JSSA learns a shared low-dimensional query–key subspace chosen to preserve token scores, fit by a tractable spectral approximation, and selects tokens in that subspace. A gather-free indexed-attention kernel lets each KV head pick different tokens under standard grouped-query attention cache layouts, so no cache reorganisation is needed. It is applied without retraining to GQA, hybrid sliding-window/global and MLA models.

**Result:** Up to 6.43x decoding throughput over full-KV attention at 128K context on an NVIDIA B200; at roughly 700B scale it reaches 96–99% of the throughput of the method it is compared with; reasoning accuracy is reported as comparable to model-native DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA), without training.

**Critique:** The abstract gives accuracy only qualitatively ('near-full accuracy'); the numbers have to come from the paper. Competing sparse-attention methods are not named in the abstract (it only says they inflate generation length and fail in architecture-dependent ways). The 6.43x is an 'up to' figure at one context length on one GPU. Code is linked (github.com/jiwonsong-dev/JSSA), but the results are not independently reproduced.

### 4. [Latent Compaction with Test-Time Training](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20582)

HF Daily Papers · paper · inference

**In one line:** Latent Compaction trains a small document-specific KV cache at test time; 96.7% on RULER at 32x compression vs 40.0% for KVZip+

**What:** KV-cache compression that optimises the compressed cache itself rather than choosing which tokens to evict. Entries are initialised by reconstruction importance, teacher attention and key-space diversity; training then (1) matches the full cache's attention outputs and log-normalisers with the reconstruction-chosen entries frozen, and (2) jointly optimises the whole cache by end-to-end distillation from the full-cache model. Model weights stay frozen, and only generic prompts about the document are used, with no downstream questions or answers.

**Result:** 96.7% RULER accuracy at 32x compression vs 40.0% for KVZip+; large budget-matched gains on RULER, LongBench and Phonebook Lookup; transfers across model scales and families (Qwen3, MiniCPM5).

**Critique:** Each document needs a test-time optimisation run, and the abstract does not report that compute or latency, which decides whether this beats simply keeping the full cache for one-off queries. KVZip+ is the only baseline named on the page. Gains are reported on retrieval-heavy benchmarks where learned compaction should shine; reasoning or generation-quality effects are not stated. Code is released (THUNLP).

### 6. [TurboMamba: Faster Inference, Faster Training, and Better Prediction with Lexical Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20682)

arXiv · paper · architecture

**In one line:** TurboMamba: Mamba-3 summarises context once per block and a small Transformer predicts tokens within the block

**What:** A block-wise hybrid. A Mamba-3 module processes global context only once per completed block of tokens; a small Transformer then predicts each next token from that context plus the earlier tokens in the current block. Three cheap additions target local prediction: access to the previous block's final token, a small adjustment to next-token logits, and a learned blend with recent-token frequencies (the 'lexical memory'). Twelve small models with comparable parameter counts were trained on FineWeb-Edu under capped budgets.

**Result:** Lowest mean test NLL of the twelve models: 9.4% below Mamba-3, 7.3% below a Mamba-3 Jamba variant and 18.3% below a Mamba-3 Zamba variant. Training throughput 2.60x Mamba-3, 2.81x Jamba, 4.82x Zamba. Batch latency (32 sequences, 256-token prompts, 128 generated tokens) 3.12x faster than Mamba-3, 3.53x than Jamba, 6.21x than Zamba.

**Critique:** Single author, small scale, and the abstract gives no model sizes. Evaluation is test NLL on one pretraining corpus with no downstream or long-context recall tasks, which is exactly where block-wise state compression is expected to hurt. The frequency-blend trick may inflate NLL gains on web text without improving capability. The latency test uses short prompts (256 tokens). The Jamba and Zamba baselines are modified re-implementations, not the originals. No code linked.

### 7. [Rethinking Routing Exploration in Modern Mixture-of-Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22353)

arXiv · paper · architecture

**In one line:** MirrorMoE argues shared experts reduce router exploration in fine-grained MoEs and suppresses unneeded routes per token

**What:** Modern MoEs combine fine-grained expert segmentation with always-on shared experts. The authors argue the two conflict: fine-grained segmentation enlarges the routing space combinatorially and so needs more exploration, while shared experts reduce the variability of token representations and make the router less exploratory, leaving capacity unused. MirrorMoE adaptively shrinks the routing search space by suppressing experts a given token does not need. Authors include Niao He and Yi Ma; the work is listed at NeurIPS 2026.

**Result:** The abstract reports only that MirrorMoE consistently improves performance across model scales; it gives no model sizes, benchmarks, baselines or numbers.

**Critique:** Without any numbers in the abstract, the size of the gain, the scales tested and the compute overhead of the suppression mechanism are unknown; read the paper before relying on it. It is also not stated whether the gains survive at the large expert counts and token budgets used by production fine-grained MoEs (DeepSeek-style), or what happens to load balance.

### 8. [Task-Phase-Conditioned Expert Routing Is Not a Reliable Proxy for Quantization Sensitivity in MoE FFN Tensors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20826)

arXiv · paper · efficiency

**In one line:** Negative result: routing statistics did not beat uniform bit allocation for mixed-precision quantization of Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B experts

**What:** Tests a common heuristic: that expert-routing statistics can tell you which MoE expert tensors tolerate fewer bits. The author profiled Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B in ik_llama.cpp by layer and task phase (prompt, reasoning, response), combined routing entropy, coefficient of variation, phase fragility and top-1 expert strength into a sensitivity score, and used coordinate descent to allocate bit-widths to routed-expert FFN tensors under a fixed average budget, with a random-allocation control.

**Result:** Across flat and steep budget tiers, routing-guided reallocation did not beat the uniform baseline; random allocation did about as well as guided allocation in the steep tier, and the aggressive-budget configuration showed a significant loss.

**Critique:** Single model, single inference stack and single author, so the conclusion may not generalise to other MoEs or quantizers. Only one family of routing-derived scores is tested; that does not rule out better sensitivity signals (e.g. Hessian- or activation-based). Including a random control and reporting a null result are both useful and uncommon. arXiv's listing page and the abstract page disagreed on the ID (2610.20824 vs 2610.20826); the URL is the abstract page that was read.
