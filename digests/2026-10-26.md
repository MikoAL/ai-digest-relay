### 1. [EAGLE-4 on AMD Instinct GPUs from day 0](https://www.amd.com/en/developer/resources/technical-articles/2026/eagle-4-on-amd-instinct-gpus.html)

AMD Developer blog · release · inference

**In one line:** EAGLE-4 speculative drafter: 289 tok/s vs 82 plain and 178 built-in MTP on Qwen3.8-27B, one MI355X, SGLang (AMD, Oct 20)

**What:** EAGLE-4 is the EAGLE team's new speculative-decoding drafter. Per AMD's day-0 post (20 Oct), it adds three things to the EAGLE family: (1) per-step tree adaptation, where a harness profiles draft and verify cost on the actual hardware at start-up and picks the draft tree that maximises accepted tokens per unit cost, so it uses wide, deep trees at batch 1 and short chains under heavy load; (2) a low-rank head that shortlists vocabulary candidates before the full LM head scores them, cutting draft cost by more than 40% without truncating the vocabulary; (3) a one-layer drafter. A secondary AlphaSignal summary adds a sliding-window draft layer and append-only draft KV cache, vLLM/SGLang integration, and a source-available licence that caps free commercial use at USD 20M annual revenue.

**Result:** Qwen3.8-27B BF16, SPEED-Bench, thinking on, greedy, one MI355X: at 1 concurrent conversation, 82 tok/s plain, 178 with built-in MTP, 289 with EAGLE-4 on SGLang and 309 on ATOM (about 3.5x plain, 1.6x MTP). At 32 conversations: 1,285 / 2,551 / 2,784 / 3,243. With 4-GPU tensor parallelism at 1 request: 156 / 303 / 463 (SGLang) / 489 (ATOM).

**Critique:** These are vendor numbers on one model, one benchmark, greedy decoding only. The advantage over built-in MTP shrinks from 1.6x at batch 1 to about 1.09x at 32 concurrent requests on SGLang. The EAGLE team's own repo, paper PDF and model card could not be retrieved this run, so the design description rests on AMD's summary and nothing is independently reproduced. The licence is source-available, not open source (per secondary coverage). The release dates from 20 Oct and had not been covered.

### 2. [How we deploy trillion-parameter frontier models on Cerebras](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/how-we-deploy-trillion-parameter-frontier-models-on-cerebras)

Cerebras blog · news · inference

**In one line:** Cerebras maps ~3T-param Kimi K3 onto 59 WSE-3T wafers as a layer pipeline, with 4-bit weights and an 8-bit KV cache

**What:** Cerebras explains how it serves Kimi K3 (about 3T parameters; 69 linear-attention layers plus 24 dense-attention layers) from on-chip SRAM. Each wafer has 44 GB of SRAM and each layer needs about 16 GB with 4-bit weights. Two linear-attention layers share a wafer (35 wafers), while each dense layer gets its own wafer (24 wafers) and uses the leftover SRAM for its 8-bit KV cache. Layers are pipeline stages; weights stay put and only activations cross wafers over RDMA-over-Ethernet at about 2 microseconds per hop. Requests stream through the pipeline continuously, and concurrency is roughly stages times micro-batch size. The ceiling comes from KV cache on the dense layers, since linear-attention layers keep a fixed-size state per request.

**Critique:** An explanatory post, not a benchmark. The '1000-2000+ tokens per second' figure is stated as a target, without saying whether it is per user or aggregate, and the concurrency figure appears only in an image. No GPU baseline, no tokens/s per megawatt despite calling it the meaningful metric, and no accuracy impact of 4-bit weights or 8-bit KV. The 256k average request length assumption drives the KV budget. Hacker News coverage headlined 2,000 tok/s, which the post does not firmly claim. Published 22 Oct; it reached the HN front page on 25 Oct.

### 3. [What Matters for NVFP4 Training? A Comparative Study of Low-Precision Pretraining Recipes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24343)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2610.24343
_Anjulie Agrusa, Andrei Panferov, Elizabeth Wei, Keith Wyss, Paul Gibbons, Erik Schultheis et al._

**In one line:** Controlled comparison of NVFP4 pretraining recipes on 8B dense and 30B-A3B MoE models up to 1T tokens; proposes a combined recipe

**What:** Most FP4 training methods have been validated only on small models and short runs. This paper compares recent NVFP4 pretraining recipes head to head on a dense 8B model and a 30B-A3B MoE, each trained for up to 1T tokens, and ablates which components are needed to stay close to BF16. The final recipe combines established stability techniques with an unbiased gradient estimator that has lower quantization error than stochastic rounding, and keeps selected layers in high precision.

**Result:** The authors claim the strongest FP4 training result at this scale to date, measured by loss gap to BF16. Ablations: every component of the recipe improves the loss trajectory, keeping some layers in high precision is necessary at scale, and de-biasing helps some backward-pass tensors more than others. The abstract gives no numeric loss gap, and the HTML tables could not be retrieved this run.

**Critique:** A 10-page workshop paper (ICML 2026 AdaptFM). The headline metric is pretraining loss gap; downstream evaluation is not described in the abstract. No end-to-end throughput or energy numbers are given, though the motivation is speed. Which layers stay in high precision, and therefore how much of the 4-bit speedup is kept, was not visible. Submitted 17 Oct; not previously covered.

### 4. [Mixed Precision Scaling Laws for Quantized Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17637)

arXiv · paper · scaling · arXiv:2610.17637
_Jinuk Kim, Rajarshi Saha, Muhammed Fatih Balın, Youngsuk Park_

**In one line:** Precision scaling law that splits forward and backward quantization and predicts optimal training precision rises with compute

**What:** The paper fits a scaling law for quantized pretraining that treats forward-pass and backward-pass precision separately. Each scheme rescales effective parameters (forward) or effective tokens (backward), and an added persistent optimization-error term per pass is motivated by a convex convergence bound. A layer-wise extension interpolates loss by the fraction of blocks in each scheme. The authors then solve for compute-optimal model size, token count and per-layer forward/backward precision (BF16, MXFP8 and NVFP4 variants), given assumed hardware speedups.

**Result:** Up to 11x lower MSE than existing precision scaling laws on held-out loss as size and tokens scale. In a 430M / 12.8B-token decomposition, NVFP4 in the backward pass only adds 0.32-0.72% loss over BF16, while NVFP4 in the forward pass only adds 0.94-1.22%. The best FP4 recipe (Quartet II, 4/6 + 1D scaling with MS-EDEN) still leaves a 1.07% gap. Unlike prior laws, it predicts that the optimal precision increases with compute budget.

**Critique:** The authors' own fits use Qwen3-style models only up to 0.9B parameters and 35.8B tokens, plus published Quartet results, so frontier-scale conclusions are extrapolated. Compute-optimal allocations depend on assumed speedups (MXFP8 fixed at 1.8x) and will move with kernels and hardware. The theory assumes convexity and bounded moments. Some scheme combinations (e.g. 2D forward scaling) are excluded from the grid. Submitted 13 Oct; not previously covered.

### 6. [Accelerating PyTorch on AMD GPUs with FlyDSL](https://pytorch.org/blog/accelerating-pytorch-on-amd-gpus-with-flydsl/)

PyTorch blog · release · efficiency

**In one line:** FlyDSL becomes an optional PyTorch backend on MI350: 1.68x geomean over ATen on MXFP4 GEMM, up to 3.01x in vLLM A4W4

**What:** FlyDSL is a ROCm Python GPU DSL built on layout algebra and lowered through MLIR. It exposes the tile, wave and thread partitioning, MFMA atoms, LDS swizzles and synchronization that Triton mostly automates. It is now wired into stock PyTorch APIs as an opt-in backend: eager-mode RMSNorm and topk dispatch to it; TorchInductor autotunes FlyDSL against ATen and Triton for dense, grouped and MXFP8/MXFP4 scaled GEMMs; and SDPA can pick FlyDSL kernels through AOTriton. Unsupported inputs fall back to the existing kernels.

**Result:** MI355X kernel geomeans: dense BF16 GEMM 1.10x over the better of ATen and Triton (15 shapes); MXFP8 scaled GEMM 1.58x over ATen; MXFP4 1.68x over ATen (range 1.31-2.96x); grouped BF16 GEMM 1.20x over Triton and 1.95x over ATen; topk (K at most 16) 4.82x. End-to-end vLLM on one MI355X: BF16 up to 1.13x (Llama-3.1-8B), MXFP8 A8W8 1.04-2.04x and MXFP4 A4W4 1.25-3.01x over the hipBLASLt baseline.

**Critique:** Vendor-authored (AMD with Meta). Limited to gfx950. The kernel timings exclude compile and autotune cost. The vLLM MXFP cells are one run each. Large MXFP gains are measured against ATen/hipBLASLt rather than tuned Triton or AITER kernels, so how much is FlyDSL versus a weak baseline is unclear. The attention numbers compare AOTriton releases and do not isolate FlyDSL. Llama-3.3-70B BF16 gains are within noise. Published 21 Oct; not previously covered.

### 7. [Orthros: Phase-Aware Heterogeneous Attention for Efficient Transformers](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/154037)

NeurIPS 2026 · paper · architecture · arXiv:2610.13735
_Yuhong Chou, Zehao Liu, Yuqi Pan, Qian Liu, Xianwei Chen, Jibin Wu_

**In one line:** Orthros uses linear-complexity attention for prefill and full attention for decode in one module; 21.9x prefill speedup claimed

**What:** Hybrid models usually interleave full-attention layers with linear or sliding-window layers to keep in-context recall. Orthros instead decouples by phase within a single attention module: prefill runs a linear-complexity computation, while decoding attends with full attention. That lowers asymptotic cost for prefill-heavy, decode-light workloads such as long-document QA and RAG. The paper also describes a data-efficient conversion of a pretrained Transformer into Orthros.

**Result:** Reports a 21.9x prefill speedup over Transformers and near-perfect 128K needle-in-a-haystack retrieval. It says the converted model surpasses the original Transformer and that real-world in-context recall is on par with strong Transformer baselines. The poster abstract gives no benchmark scores or named baseline models.

**Critique:** Only the NeurIPS poster abstract could be read this run; the arXiv page was rate-limited, so the mechanism (how decode-time full attention reuses a prefill that never built a full KV cache) and the model sizes are unverified. NIAH is a weak test of long-context ability. The 21.9x is versus vanilla attention, not FlashAttention-class kernels or other hybrids. Decode-phase memory and cost are not quantified. From the 12 Oct batch; not previously covered.

### 8. [FIGFLEX: A Mixed-Precision FP-INT GEMM Accelerator Co-Designed with Low-Bit LLM Quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30480)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2610.30480
_Faraz Tahmasebi, Haocheng Xu, Andrew Trauger, Yomal Perera, Zhenyu Tang, Nael Chowdhury et al._

**In one line:** FIGFLEX co-designs a mixed-precision FP-INT GEMM accelerator and quantizer: 1.56x throughput/area over AxCore at 3-bit

**What:** Weight-only quantization saves memory but not compute: GPUs dequantize to FP16, and bit-parallel FP-INT accelerators support only one weight precision. FIGFLEX reformulates FP-INT multiplication as radix-4 shift-add, merging multiply and accumulate into one partial-product reduction and roughly halving partial products, and uses spare datapath at INT3. A matching quantizer assigns bit-widths per channel group from the error GPTQ cannot compensate, and applies power-of-two column scaling so activations stay out of FP16 subnormals.

**Result:** LLaMA-2-7B WikiText-2 perplexity without fine-tuning: 5.85 at 3-bit average and 8.77 at 2-bit average, reported as better than GPTQ, AWQ, SliM-LLM, AMQ and QuIP#. Whole-chip throughput per area on the linear GEMMs of a 3-bit mixed-precision model: 1.56x over AxCore and 1.42x over FIGLUT.

**Critique:** Accuracy is shown only on LLaMA-2-7B perplexity, an old model and a narrow metric, with no downstream tasks in the abstract. The abstract does not say whether the hardware results come from silicon or from synthesis and simulation, and they cover GEMMs only, not attention or end-to-end latency. Baseline perplexities were not visible. Mixed per-group bit-widths complicate integration into real serving stacks. Submitted 21 Oct; not previously covered.
