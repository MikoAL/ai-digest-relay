### 1. [Channel Selection for Mixture-of-Experts Decoding Needs No Calibration Data](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37386)

HF Daily Papers · paper · inference · arXiv:2610.37386
_Geunhyoung Kim, Sung Ju Hwang_

**In one line:** Calibration-free MoE channel reader keeps 98.1% accuracy at 17.8% of routed-expert reads; 1.60-1.90x faster MoE blocks than vLLM at bs=1

**What:** Small-batch MoE decoding is bound by loading routed-expert weights, so channel selectors read only the channels predicted to matter, normally via a threshold calibrated on offline data. The authors show a calibrated threshold does not control how many channels are actually read (it varies per input), and that a per-token top-k rule needs no calibration and usually does at least as well. They then build a reader from model weights alone: a ternary sketch screens channels and a low-rank map recovers the contribution of skipped ones, with Triton kernels for the MoE block.

**Result:** Thresholds calibrated for an 18% read budget actually read about 12.9% on average. Top-k matches or beats the calibrated threshold in 40 of 43 comparisons at 13% and 18% budgets (all 23 generation tasks). The weight-only reader retains 98.1% of full-model accuracy at 17.8% of routed-expert reads across three post-trained MoE models; MoE blocks run 1.60-1.90x faster than vLLM at batch size 1 and up to 2.69x at larger batches. On Gemma 4, calibrated thresholds caused repetition-loop collapse that top-k avoided.

**Critique:** Read from the Hugging Face paper page (abstract-level numbers); arXiv was rate-limited, so per-benchmark tables, the three model names and the GPU used were not checked. Speedups are for the MoE block only, not end-to-end decode latency. 98.1% is an average retention and can hide large drops on individual tasks. Comparison is to threshold-based selectors and vLLM; other sparse-expert-loading methods may not be covered.

### 2. [Multi-State Linear Attention with Generalized Second-Order Routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37645)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2610.37645
_Hee Seung Hwang, Sanghyuk Chun, Xindi Wu, Olga Russakovsky, Zhiwei Deng_

**In one line:** MERLIN routes queries across multi-state linear-attention memories using key mean and variance; +13.5% LongBench on Gated DeltaNet-1.3B

**What:** Multi-State Linear Attention (MSLA) splits the context into segments, compresses each into its own recurrent memory state, and routes queries to states by the mean query-key alignment within a segment, ignoring how that alignment varies inside the segment. MERLIN additionally tracks the first and second moments of each segment's keys, so a query can recover both the mean and the variance of its alignment with every segment and weight the per-state readouts accordingly.

**Result:** On multi-state Gated DeltaNet-1.3B and Mamba-2-780M, MERLIN improves over existing aggregation methods by 8.3% and 7.0% on RULER needle-in-a-haystack and long-context QA, and by 13.5% and 12.0% on LongBench; the abstract says it also beats baselines that use 4x the memory.

**Critique:** Numbers are from the abstract: the baseline aggregation methods are not named and it is not stated whether gains are relative or absolute. Scale is small (0.78B-1.3B). Throughput and memory cost of storing second-moment statistics per segment are not given in the abstract, and there is no comparison against full-attention or hybrid models at equal compute.

### 3. [Truncate or Not? A Scaling Study of Looped Transformers with Truncated Backpropagation](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26030)

arXiv · paper · scaling · arXiv:2610.26030
_Xuyuan Xiong, Siyu Chen, Zihan Shao, Yue Su, Weiyang Jin, Ping Luo et al._

**In one line:** Scaling law over ~592 looped-transformer runs: at matched compute, full BPTT through fewer loops beats truncated backprop

**What:** Looped transformers reuse a shared block for extra depth, and truncated BPTT (backprop through only the last B of T loops) cuts training compute but biases gradients. The authors fit a joint scaling law in model size, tokens and (T, B), counting the recurrent block's parameters times a saturating looping coefficient g(T,B)=exp[c(1-T^-p B^-q)] (fitted c~1.11, p~0.24, q~1.02). The suite spans 57.8M-918.7M non-embedding params, 1.1-1,291 tokens per parameter on FineWeb-Edu, and T in {1,2,4,8,16}.

**Result:** Fit R^2=0.9992 with in-sample RMSE 6.14e-3 nats/token and leave-one-(T,B)-out RMSE 8.67e-3 (fit on 230 configs, 117 held out). With forward and backward loops optimized jointly, the law favors full BPTT. A (2,2) looped model at 344M params reaches 2.36 nats vs 2.37 for a 471M non-looped model: ~27% fewer non-embedding params for ~8% more training compute. When model size is free, the law favors no looping under its leading-FLOP approximation.

**Critique:** Dense models up to ~0.9B on one dataset and one architecture; MoE and larger scales are untested. Only loss is fitted, not downstream reasoning where looping is usually claimed to help. The free-size conclusion depends on a leading-FLOP approximation that ignores memory and wall-clock effects of looping. Held-out (T,B) errors are larger than other splits. The page read is v2 (20 Oct); no code link was found.

### 4. [On the SPOT: Enhancing Speculative Decoding for Diffusion Language Models via Commit-Order Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22543)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2610.22543
_Eliezer Shlomi, Eliron Rahimi, Avi Mendelson, Chaim Baskin_

**In one line:** SPOT: 0.62M-param drafter predicts which positions a diffusion LM fills next; up to 6.8x fewer target passes, 3.2x throughput

**What:** Exact diffusion-LM decoding fills one masked position per pass, while multi-token methods are lossy; standard speculative decoding does not transfer because the draft must choose both token and position. The authors observe the target's previous-pass predictions already contain the correct token at 92-100% of positions, so what is missing is the fill order. SPOT trains a 0.62M-parameter drafter that reads only the target's output distributions to predict that order, proposes a tree of continuations verified in one target pass, and uses coupled-noise (shared-Gumbel) match verification so it is lossless at any temperature.

**Result:** Up to 6.8x fewer target passes and up to 3.2x throughput over standard decoding; outperforms calibrated and training-free drafting baselines on every task and budget at T=0 and T=1. The drafter transfers across six diffusion LMs.

**Critique:** Numbers are 'up to' figures from the abstract; baselines and per-task results are not named there and were not checked because the full text was rate-limited. Throughput gains depend on the base DLM's cost per pass and are not compared to an autoregressive model of similar quality. Submitted 15 Oct and surfaced on HF Daily Papers 28 Oct; not covered in earlier digests.

### 5. [Serving DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at Scale: vLLM Matches DeepInfer and Scales to GB300 NVL72](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-23-deepseek-v41-flash-pd-wide-ep)

vLLM blog · news · inference

**In one line:** vLLM serves DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at 954K tok/s per GPU on one GB300 NVL72 with prefill/decode disaggregation and wide expert parallelism

**What:** Engineering write-up on serving DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (384 routed experts, top-6; FP4 KV compression and cross-layer KV sharing) on one GB300 NVL72 rack with P/D disaggregation: DEP2 prefill and decode scaled from DEP4 to DEP32, EPLB rebalancing for target and DSpark draft models, and CPU-overhead fixes (NIXL push connector, recursive-doubling CPU all-reduce, admission caps).

**Critique:** Vendor-authored (vLLM/Inferact) with synthetic or replayed AgentX traffic. The 954K figure counts cached input, uncached input and output tokens together. The DeepInfer comparison reads values off published charts with different prefill configs, and the decode comparison uses GB200 while the headline uses GB300. Posted 23 Oct.

### 6. [Cloudflare: cross-customer KV-cache exposure in GLM-5.3 on Workers AI traced to SGLang disaggregated-prefill bug](https://blog.cloudflare.com/kv-cache-exposure-remediation/)

Cloudflare blog · news · inference

**In one line:** Cloudflare: SGLang P/D bug plus undersized prefill KV pool let 155 GLM-5.3 responses attend over other requests' stale KV pages

**What:** GLM-5.3 ran with a 1,048,576-token context but a prefill KV pool of only 681,280 tokens (SGLang sizes it from leftover GPU memory). In disaggregated mode, over-length prompts were marked rejected and swapped for a one-token placeholder but not aborted; admission checked finished(), so they were admitted, prefill shipped one KV page, and decode reserved more pages than were written and attended over stale pages holding earlier requests' data, returning HTTP 200. Fixes: a ~2.04M-token prefill pool and rejection at admission before KV transfer (upstream SGLang PRs #36988 and #38935; v0.5.22+ recommended).

**Result:** 155 affected responses, all with prompts over 681,280 tokens, between 27 Aug and 9 Sep 2026; Cloudflare says no other Workers AI model was affected.

**Critique:** Self-reported postmortem; the affected-response count and the claim that other models were unaffected are not independently verified. Other SGLang deployments using P/D disaggregation with a KV pool smaller than the context length may have had the same exposure, which the post does not quantify. Published 26 Oct.

### 8. [Next-Concept Modeling: Fast, Parallel Coarse-to-Fine Text Generation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14162)

HF Trending Papers · paper · architecture · arXiv:2610.14162
_Hermann Kumbong, Ishan S. Khare, Iddah Mlauzi, Andrew Woen, Charles Ding, E. Kelly Buchanan et al._

**In one line:** Next-Concept Modeling generates text coarse-to-fine over residual-VQ code levels; 21x faster than strongest DLM on LM1B

**What:** Diffusion LMs sample in parallel but degrade with fewer steps and process the full sequence every step. NCM first encodes text with a multiscale residual VQ-VAE into a hierarchy of discrete codes, then a transformer generates one level at a time, predicting all codes within a level in parallel. Code, checkpoints and datasets are released (HazyResearch).

**Result:** Versus the strongest diffusion-LM baseline at matched parameter scale: better generative perplexity at 21x speed on LM1B, competitive quality at 10x on OpenWebText, matching ROUGE-L at 31x on XSum; outperforms AR and diffusion baselines on Sudoku with fewer function evaluations.

**Critique:** Posted 9 Oct and trending on Hugging Face this week; not covered in earlier digests. The abstract does not name the 'strongest DLM baseline' or give absolute numbers; generative perplexity is a weak quality metric and 'competitive' on OpenWebText suggests a quality gap. Small benchmarks (LM1B, OWT, XSum); no comparison with an optimized autoregressive model on wall-clock latency.
