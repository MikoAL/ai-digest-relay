### 1. [Thinking Machines Inkling 2 Mini (open weights, looped MoE transformer)](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)

Thinking Machines Lab blog · release · architecture

**In one line:** Thinking Machines releases open-weight Inkling 2 Mini: looped MoE, 26.4B total / 3.4B active, middle two 6-layer blocks run 3x per token

**What:** 24-layer multimodal decoder with sparse MoE FFNs (8 of 128 experts per layer) and hybrid attention (five sliding-window layers with up to 1,024-token windows plus one full-attention layer per six-layer block). The middle two six-layer blocks (layers 6-17) are weight-shared and run three times per token, so 24 stored layers give 48 layer applications; passes 2 and 3 read a gated 'message' from the previous pass. The model card puts this at ~5.4B effective active parameters per token, with up to 1M context and an Apache 2.0 license (both per the model card page, not the blog). Training: started with 384 experts per layer and pruned to the 128 most-used in midtraining, then 'healed' with 1T tokens distilled from a larger internal Inkling; SFT plus RL totalling 2.2T tokens over 2,000 steps; NVFP4 expert layers from pretraining through RL (last 4 layers MXFP8) and deployed at that precision. The checkpoint is ~20GB and targets one DGX Spark or Apple silicon machines with 32GB+. Weights at huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini; a DSpark drafter for speculative decoding is included.

**Result:** Vendor table against Qwen3.8-27B: DeepSWE v1.1 48.2% vs 42.2%, SWE-bench Pro v2 Hard 77.9% vs 73.8%, Terminal Bench v2.1 71.6% vs 73.0%, AA Index v5.0 20.1 vs 20.1, GPQA Diamond 86.6% vs 90.5%. The loop ablation says a non-looped model needed 7.5% less training compute but 140% more inference compute to match the looped loss. 'Intelligence per joule' on DGX Spark: 0.703 vs 0.430 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B.

**Critique:** All numbers are self-reported, and agentic benchmarks depend on the harness (mini-swe-agent, pi v0.83.0). The headline comparison is against dense 27-31B models, while the closest MoE peers appear only in the energy test. The ablation is not cleanly isolated: the looped and non-looped models also differ in expert pruning and distillation, and pretraining token count and GPU-hours are not disclosed. Looping triples middle-layer FLOPs per token, so latency against a 3B-active non-looped MoE needs independent measurement. The model trails Qwen3.8-27B on knowledge-heavy tasks (AA-Omniscience -8.6 vs -6.4). Parts of the model card were read only as a summary.

### 2. [FrontierReplay: Split-Free Exact Prefix Caching for Hybrid Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26020)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.26020
_Zekun Wang, Yizhuo Wang_

**In one line:** FrontierReplay: exact prefix caching for hybrid attention-recurrent LLMs via state-only replay, up to 4.26x goodput

**What:** In hybrid models, the recurrent layers' state at an arbitrary prefix boundary is not stored, so prefix caching needs either split prefill (slow) or approximate replay. FrontierReplay instead materializes exact checkpoints at structural boundaries without splitting prefill. It logs the recurrent layers' inputs and reconstructs states with a state-only replay kernel, and keeps the retained data in slots shared with the attention KV cache.

**Result:** Replay is '6-37x cheaper per token than full recomputation'. Against existing exact caches: median TTFT down 16-89% (up to 8.9x), 80%-SLO capacity up to 1.73x, goodput up to 4.26x (3.67x at larger scales). Against approximate replay: up to 1.54x higher goodput.

**Critique:** The abstract does not name the baseline systems or the hybrid models tested. Numbers rest on the listing abstract; the abstract page was not read. Gains depend on prefix-reuse rates in the workload. The memory cost of logging recurrent inputs, compared with storing states, is not quantified in the abstract.

### 3. [DTS: Delta-Based Tile Scoring for Efficient Long-Context Sparse Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25938)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.25938
_Emre Uğur, Jiawen Qi, Qinyu Chen_

**In one line:** DTS: training-free block-sparse attention scoring; 3.3x prefill over FlashAttention-2 at 512K, within 0.1 pt of full attention

**What:** Block-sparse prefill needs a cheap way to score which key tiles matter; the scoring itself becomes a bottleneck at long context. DTS picks anchor tokens from local changes (deltas) between consecutive vectors and scores tiles against those anchors in a content-adaptive way, without training. Parallel delta packing speeds up anchor selection.

**Result:** On LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, RULER (up to 128K) and LongBench accuracy is 'within 0.10 percentage points of full attention'. Scoring-pipeline latency is 38% lower than XAttention at 128K. Prefill is 3.3x faster than a PyTorch full-attention baseline using FlashAttention-2 at 512K tokens on four H100s.

**Critique:** Only one 8B model is evaluated. The end-to-end speedup is measured against a PyTorch full-attention baseline, not against other sparse methods (XAttention, MInference, FlexPrefill); the XAttention comparison covers scoring latency only. Decode is not addressed. Details rest on the listing abstract.

### 4. [HiSA: Sparsifying the Hidden Dimension of RoPE Attention in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26363)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.26363
_Ambroise Borbely, Mathieu Salzmann, Olivier Verscheure_

**In one line:** HiSA computes only about a third of query-key products, using a static RoPE-derived sparsity pattern over the head dimension

**What:** Instead of sparsifying over tokens, Hidden Sparse Attention sparsifies the hidden (head) dimension of the query-key dot product. It uses a static, parameter-free pattern derived from RoPE's frequency structure, so each query-key pair uses only a subset of dimensions. Models are trained from scratch with this partial dot product, and FlashAttention-3 kernels are extended to support it.

**Result:** Computes 'only about a third of the query-key products' relative to the full dot product, and the authors report it 'improves decoding speed and shrinks memory footprint'. The abstract gives no absolute speed, memory or quality numbers.

**Critique:** Without numbers in the abstract, the quality cost against dense attention and the real kernel speedup cannot be judged from the listing. It requires training from scratch, so it cannot be retrofitted onto existing checkpoints. Model scale is not stated. The abstract page could not be read (fetch permission not answered).

### 5. [NariQ: Sub-3-Bit Quantization for Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26157)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.26157
_Doyeob Kim_

**In one line:** NariQ: trellis-coded PTQ calibrated on agent sessions matches BF16 on DeepSWE and Terminal Bench at 3.0 and 2.5 bits per weight

**What:** Low-bit quantization errors compound over long agentic trajectories. NariQ uses trellis-coded post-training quantization, calibrated on tokens produced by the BF16 model during real agent sessions instead of generic text.

**Result:** Prior quantizations at 3 bits or below 'solve at most 72% as many tasks as BF16 on DeepSWE'. NariQ at 3.0 and 2.5 bits per weight 'stays level with BF16' on DeepSWE and Terminal Bench. The 2.0-bit checkpoint trails.

Gains may come mostly from in-domain calibration data rather than the trellis code; an ablation would separate the two. The abstract page could not be read.

### 6. [Complete-SVD: A Functional Norm Approach to Low-Rank LLM Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26059)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.26059
_Alper Kalle, Theo Rudkiewicz, Mohamed-Oumar Ouerfelli_

**In one line:** Complete-SVD: post-training low-rank LLM compression that minimizes output-logit distance via Kronecker-factored weighted SVD

**What:** SVD-based compression usually minimizes per-layer weight or activation error. Complete-SVD instead targets the L2 distance between the original and compressed model's logits, solved as a weighted SVD with a Kronecker-factored approximation of the weighting so it remains tractable.

**Result:** With WikiText-2 calibration it 'achieves the lowest C4 and PTB perplexity in every setting' and improves IFEval accuracy over 'the strongest prior output-aware methods'. The abstract gives no specific numbers.

**Critique:** No numbers, compression ratios or model names in the abstract. Perplexity-centric evaluation with one instruction-following benchmark. Low-rank compression usually lags quantization at equal memory, and that comparison is not mentioned. Details rest on the listing abstract.

### 7. [AMD Nitro-P: VAE-free pixel-space text-to-image diffusion (679M)](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/artificial-intelligence/nitro-p/README.html)

AMD ROCm blog · release · efficiency

**In one line:** AMD releases Nitro-P, a 679M VAE-free pixel-space T2I model: GenEval 0.88 at 10.26 samples/s (512px) on one MI300X

**What:** Two-stage pixel-space diffusion without a VAE. An E-MMDiT on 16x16 RGB patches (Qwen3-1.7B text encoder) produces a 1/16-resolution semantic map, which modulates a lightweight U-Net pixel decoder through spatially varying scale and shift at the bottleneck. Training uses frequency-aware (DCT) flow matching, REPA alignment to DINOv2, and adversarial plus diffusion distillation for a one-step variant. Data: 25M public real and synthetic pairs, with a 256-to-1024px curriculum on MI300X. Weights at huggingface.co/amd/Nitro-P, code at github.com/AMD-AGI/Nitro-P.

**Result:** 512px, single MI300X, batch 32: GenEval 0.88 at 10.26 samples/s, vs PixelDiT 0.78 at 5.54, DeCo 0.86 at 6.13, PixelGen 0.79 at 7.6, MiniT2I-L 0.88 at 2.4. The one-step variant gets 0.85 at 90.02 samples/s. 1024px: 0.87 vs PixelDiT 0.74, at 2.44 vs 2.05 samples/s.

**Critique:** All numbers are vendor-measured on AMD hardware only, with GenEval as the main quality metric (no FID or human preference). It ties MiniT2I-L on GenEval at 512px. At 20 steps it is slower than AMD's own Nitro-E (10.26 vs 18.83 samples/s). Total training compute and the license are not stated. FLUX-generated synthetic data (9.5M pairs) may favour GenEval-style prompts.

### 9. [Approximate Adjoint Models for Memory and Compute-Efficient Online Learning of Hybrid Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26095)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.26095
_Emilio González-Zamora, Pierre Tandeo, Laurent Debreu_

**In one line:** Approximate adjoints cut peak memory up to 10x and runtime up to 4x for online training of hybrid physics-ML models

**What:** Training a neural component embedded in a long physical simulation needs backpropagation through many time steps, usually done with truncated BPTT. This method computes exact pullbacks only at sparse anchor states and propagates gradients between anchors with zero-order or pullback-reuse approximations.

**Result:** 'Peak memory falls by up to an order of magnitude and runtime up to fourfold' relative to the standard approach (truncated BPTT), per the abstract.

**Critique:** Geared to physics/ML hybrid models, so transfer to LLM training (where activation checkpointing is the standard comparison) is unclear. The accuracy cost of the approximate gradient is not quantified in the listing abstract, and test systems and scale are not stated there.

### 11. [Game-Theoretic Data Allocation for Expert Specialization and Routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25167)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.25167
_Lovett, Lim, Vosoughi_

**In one line:** InflEns: mixture of LoRA experts where data allocation from a location game replaces a learned router

**What:** Rather than training a gating network, InflEns assigns training data to LoRA experts by solving a location game over data influence, so each expert specializes on a share of the data. At inference, routing follows that allocation, with no learned gate.

**Result:** The expert with the largest data share has the lowest NLL on about 90% of prompts, versus about 15% when allocations are permuted. Retraining a single expert lowers HarmBench attack success from 0.51 to 0.02.

**Critique:** No comparison with learned-router MoE or standard multi-LoRA routing on task quality or efficiency in the abstract. The specialization metric is internal (NLL share), and the HarmBench result shows modularity rather than efficiency. Scale and base model are not stated. Author list is surnames from the listing summary.
