### 3. [LongCat Recall Sparse Attention: Co-Designing Key Organization and Retrieval for Long-Context Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27233)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.27233
_Hao Zhang, Bin Xiao, Kefeng Zhang_

**In one line:** LRSA: retrieval-based sparse attention with a paged incremental key index; 4.70x faster key selection than DSA at 1M tokens

**What:** Long-context sparse attention normally pays a growing cost to score and select keys each step. LRSA co-designs how keys are organized and retrieved: it maintains an incrementally updated, paged index over keys so the selection step retrieves candidate blocks instead of scoring every key. It is integrated into SGLang.

**Result:** At a 1024K-token context: key selection 4.70x faster than DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA); selection plus attention combined 35.60x faster than full attention.

**Critique:** The listing abstract reports kernel/latency speedups only; accuracy versus DSA and full attention on long-context benchmarks was not visible in what was read. Speedups are at a single extreme length (1M); behavior at 32K-128K, where most serving happens, is not stated. Hardware and batch settings unknown; abstract page could not be read.

### 4. [MemOnce: A Scalable Memory Architecture for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27973)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · inference

**In one line:** MemOnce caches a subset of final-layer states and decodes them into KV caches on demand for long-context memory

**What:** Instead of storing per-layer KV caches for the full context, MemOnce caches only a small subset of final-layer hidden states and decodes them back into KV caches for the attention layers when needed, with sparse retrieval indices computed once and shared across layers. Details beyond the listing come from AI Weekly's summary of the paper.

**Result:** As reported by AI Weekly: on Qwen3.5-4B at 1M tokens, state cache 1313x smaller (about 25 MB vs over 32 GB for a vanilla Transformer), prefill 6.9x faster, throughput 3.5x higher; memory-benchmark average 60.07 vs 54.30 for full-context Qwen3.5-4B across MemBench, LoCoMo, LongMemEval and two PersonaMem splits; retrieval 64.2 ms, 12x faster than M+ and 17x faster than RAG.

**Critique:** Numbers come from secondary coverage; the arXiv abstract page and HF paper page could not be read. Evaluation is on conversational-memory benchmarks, not needle/retrieval-heavy long-context tasks where compressing to final-layer states could lose detail. Pretraining sweeps stop at 1.0B parameters on 8 H100s; single-team preprint with no independent reproduction.

### 5. [PLoQ: A Prompt-Specific Low-Rank Basis for 4-bit Quantization of LLM Activations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27092)

arXiv cs.LG · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.27092
_Faraz Tahmasebi, Michael Pellauer, Hyoukjun Kwon_

**In one line:** PLoQ: per-prompt low-rank activation basis for W4A4KV4 quantization recovers up to 46% of lost perplexity, Llama/Qwen 1B-70B

**What:** Activation outliers make 4-bit activation and KV quantization lossy. PLoQ estimates a low-rank activation basis per prompt during prefill, quantizes that component separately and restores it inside the GEMM, without changing the weights. One-eighth of channels are kept at 8 bits, for about 4.2 bits per activation on average.

**Result:** Outperforms the state-of-the-art mixed-precision quantization on six Llama and Qwen models (1B-70B) at up to 8k tokens (16k on two models), recovering up to 46% of the perplexity that method loses. Overhead on Llama-3.1-8B at 8k tokens: +4% prefill, +8% decode.

**Critique:** The baseline is described only as 'state-of-the-art mixed-precision quantization' in the abstract; comparison against rotation methods (QuaRot/SpinQuant-style) not visible. Results are perplexity-centric; downstream task accuracy and long-generation quality unclear. Overhead is measured against what baseline kernel is unstated, and context tops out at 16k.

### 6. [Accelerating Muon with Spectral Residual Correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27077)

arXiv cs.LG · paper · training · arXiv:2611.27077
_Zhixin Ren, Yao Lyu, Shengbo Eben Li_

**In one line:** AIMuon adds a gradient-momentum residual to Muon's orthogonalized step; 6.5-10% fewer steps to Muon's loss on GPT-2/Qwen3-0.6B

**What:** Muon replaces the momentum update with its matrix sign (orthogonalization), discarding spectral magnitude information. AIMuon corrects the spectral step with a residual between gradient and momentum, approximating both matrix-sign transforms with Newton-Schulz iterations, and gives a stochastic stationarity bound.

**Result:** Reaches Muon's final validation loss with 8.98% (GPT-2-small), 10.05% (GPT-2-medium) and 6.49% (Qwen3-0.6B) fewer steps, and reaches a lower final loss than Muon, Muon-Nesterov and MONA.

**Critique:** Small scale (≤0.6B), so gains may not survive at frontier scale where Muon tuning differs. Step counts, not wall-clock: a second Newton-Schulz transform adds per-step cost that is not reported in the abstract. No AdamW comparison mentioned; seeds and hyperparameter sweeps unknown.

### 7. [Distillation with Errors: Sparse Unbiased Backpropagation for Knowledge Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27131)

arXiv cs.LG · paper · training · arXiv:2611.27131
_Yuyang Zhang, Yuxin Li_

**In one line:** DWE: sparse unbiased gradient estimates for full-vocabulary KD; up to 2.20x output-head speedup, 10-16% less training time

**What:** Full-vocabulary logit distillation makes the output head and its backward pass expensive. DWE builds sparse but unbiased paired gradient estimates for the KD loss, so only a subset of vocabulary entries is backpropagated while keeping the gradient unbiased.

**Result:** At equal budget, hidden-state and output-weight gradient MSE fall 63.5% and 57.9% versus importance sampling. Off-policy KD: up to 1.95x output-head speedup and 10.4-15.7% lower training time. On-policy KD: up to 2.20x output-head speedup.

**Critique:** Speedups are for the output head; end-to-end gains (10-16%) are modest and depend on vocabulary-to-model size ratio. The abstract reports gradient error and speed but no final student-quality numbers versus full KD or top-k truncated KD. Model sizes are not stated in the listing.

### 8. [DPMA: Learning to Compress Mixture-of-Experts Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27759)

arXiv cs.LG · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.27759
_Jon Irureta, Jon Imaz, Philipp Wiesner_

**In one line:** DPMA learns prune/merge/absorb coefficients and per-layer expert budgets; beats best baseline by up to 6.7 pts at 75% expert cut

**What:** MoE models are memory-heavy because all experts must be resident. DPMA is a retraining-free compressor with frozen weights that learns, per expert, coefficients for pruning, merging and absorbing into remaining experts, plus a layer-wise expert allocation under a global expert budget.

**Result:** Across four MoE models, eight multiple-choice benchmarks and five compression ratios; at 75% expert reduction it beats the strongest baseline on every model, by up to 6.7 percentage points.

**Critique:** Evaluation is multiple-choice only; generation, coding and long-context quality after compressing 75% of experts is untested in what was read. Which four MoE models and which baselines is not stated in the listing abstract. Memory savings and latency are not quantified.

### 9. [CoPyro: Feeding Copy Proposals to Parallel Drafters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27217)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.27217
_Hong-Kai Zheng, Piji Li_

**In one line:** CoPyro feeds n-gram copied tokens to parallel speculative drafters instead of masks; +1-5% acceptance length on Qwen3

**What:** Parallel (multi-token) drafters predict several future tokens from mask placeholders. CoPyro replaces those masks with n-gram copy proposals taken from the context, giving the drafter concrete candidate tokens to refine.

**Result:** On Qwen3-4B across eight benchmarks, acceptance length rises for all four parallel drafters tested: +2.19-5.08% at 100K training samples, +1.08-2.00% with DFlash trained on 710K samples. Overhead on Qwen3-8B: 0.24 ms per decoding round in Transformers.

**Critique:** Gains are small and shrink as drafters are trained on more data, suggesting diminishing value for well-trained drafters. Reported in acceptance length, not end-to-end tokens/s. Overhead measured in HF Transformers, not an optimized serving engine. Only Qwen3 4B/8B.

### 10. [cmHC: Balancing Stream Mixing in Hyper-Connections via Geometric Contraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27193)

arXiv cs.LG · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.27193
_Haowei He, Ziwen Zhao, Chenshuo Pan_

**In one line:** cmHC contracts mHC's Birkhoff-polytope mixing toward its interior to avoid under/over-mixing; up to 44.8% lower latency on Ascend

**What:** Manifold-constrained hyper-connections (mHC) replace the single residual stream with several streams mixed by doubly-stochastic matrices. The paper analyzes failure modes of under-mixing and over-mixing and constrains the mixing matrices to a contracted region of the Birkhoff polytope, away from its vertices. Fused operators are provided for Ascend NPUs.

**Result:** Fused operators reduce hyper-connection execution latency by up to 44.8% on Ascend NPUs; experiments on dense and MoE models at various scales.

**Critique:** The listing abstract gives no model-quality numbers versus mHC or standard residuals, and model scales are unspecified. The latency figure covers only the hyper-connection ops, not end-to-end training step time, and is Ascend-specific. Abstract page could not be read.

### 11. [DistP: Scaling Local-Update Training with Zero-Shot Hyperparameter Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27808)

**In one line:** DistP extends zero-shot hyperparameter transfer to DiLoCo-style local-update training; -8.1% val loss at 823M params

**What:** muP-style hyperparameter transfer assumes standard data-parallel training.
