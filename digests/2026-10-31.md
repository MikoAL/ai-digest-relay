### 1. [From Tokens to Bytes I: Scaling Hierarchical Networks](https://www.cartesia.ai/blog/from-tokens-to-bytes-part-1)

Cartesia blog · news · architecture

**In one line:** Cartesia: byte-level H-Nets reach BPE Transformer loss with 22% less compute; 3.67B 2-stage H-Net compared with SmolLM3-3B

**What:** Cartesia Research (28 Oct) reports scaling experiments for H-Nets, which replace a fixed tokenizer with learned end-to-end chunking of raw bytes. A ladder of BPE Transformers (221M to 2.62B params) was each converted to a 1-stage byte-level H-Net. A larger internal 2-stage H-Net (3.67B total) uses Mamba-2 outer stages plus a sliding-window attention block, around a 40-layer Transformer main network shaped like SmolLM3's; it is compute-matched to SmolLM3-3B within 2% FLOPs per byte, and its main network sees about one position per 7.6 bytes.

**Result:** Ladder: H-Net matches BPE validation loss with 22% less compute (1.28x), with larger downstream multipliers (~1.83x Paloma, ~2.06x OLMES). Code: median 2.04x compute multiplier across 15 in-distribution languages and 3.10x across 10 out-of-distribution languages. The 3.67B model is reported to reach SmolLM3's final average benchmark score after ~1T tokens vs ~11T for SmolLM3.

**Critique:** Company blog post, not peer-reviewed; no weights or code released. The SmolLM3 comparison rests on the authors' own reproduction of the recipe and their own evaluation choices, and final benchmark numbers for the larger model are only shown as curves. The authors note that fair data/compute matching between byte-level and BPE models is an open problem, the 2-stage model is an internal variant rather than the published H-Net, and no inference speed or cost is reported.

### 2. [Multi-Token Prediction Done Right](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38331)

arXiv / HF Daily Papers · paper · training · arXiv:2610.38331
_Jayden Teoh_

**In one line:** Soft-MTP: one-line change keeps only the KL self-distillation part of the multi-token prediction gradient; tested 167M–1.2B

**What:** Decomposes the standard multi-token prediction (MTP) cross-entropy gradient into a KL self-distillation term (pulling each MTP head toward the model's own next-token distribution) and a noisy residual that the paper says makes up ~80% of the MTP gradient reaching the shared trunk. A straight-through gradient-recovery trick obtains the KL gradient from existing CE gradients without materializing vocabulary-sized logit matrices; Soft-MTP keeps only that KL signal. Setup: DeepSeek-style sequential MTP heads (K=3), LLaMA-style models 167M–1.2B trained with Muon on ~31.5B FineWeb-Edu tokens.

**Result:** At 1.2B: FineWeb-Edu perplexity 10.74 vs 10.89 (standard MTP) and 10.57 (next-token-only); zero-shot average 57.31 vs 56.80 (MTP) and 56.75 (NTP); self-speculative decoding 1.76x vs 1.74x. After code fine-tuning, HumanEval pass@64 43.90 vs 40.85 (MTP) and 39.02 (NTP). Throughput on one H200 matches standard MTP (49.7k tok/s), while direct KL-MTP is slower (47.0k tok/s) and uses 128 GB vs 107 GB peak memory.

**Critique:** Single author, small scale (≤1.2B, ~31.5B tokens); frontier-scale behaviour and parallelism compatibility are argued, not measured. Soft-MTP still has worse FineWeb-Edu perplexity than plain next-token training at every size, gains over MTP are small (≈0.1–0.5 points zero-shot), and it is worse than MTP on LAMBADA at 167M and 332M. Direct KL-MTP beats it on GSM8K and MATH-500. The KL target is biased toward the model's NTP distribution rather than the data distribution, as the paper acknowledges.

### 3. [How to Loop Your Transformer: Loop Scaling, Specialization, and Parallel Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40325)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2610.40325
_Chanakya Ekbote, Artidoro Pagnoni, Devendra Singh Sachan, Shruti Bhosale, Mike Lewis, Maryam Fazel-Zarandi et al._

**In one line:** Study of looped dense Transformers up to 1B params, 155B tokens and 32 loops: full-model vs middle-block looping and parallel inference

**What:** Systematic study of weight-shared looping in dense decoder-only Transformers (adding depth without parameters). Compares looping the full model vs only a middle block, adds small loop-specific low-rank adapters, and tests Parallel Loop Transformers, which aim to remove the decode slowdown that grows with loop count. Proposes a modified design that preserves the current-token embedding and normalizes it separately from the previous-token hidden state.

**Result:** Per the abstract: looping the full Transformer gives the best quality at fixed parameter count, while looping only a middle block is better under limited training FLOPs; extra loops can plateau, reverse or destabilize; loop-specific LoRA adapters help more as loops increase; Parallel Loop Transformers did not consistently keep the quality gains in these runs, and the modified embedding-preserving design recovers strong performance across scales and budgets. Specific perplexity/accuracy numbers could not be read this run.

**Critique:** Only the abstract could be read (arXiv rate-limited the full text), so the size of the gains, baselines and compute accounting are unverified here. Scale tops out at 1B parameters, and decode latency of looped models grows with loop count, so the practical efficiency case depends on the parallel-inference variant whose results are mixed. No code link on the abstract page.

DiffuSpace blog · release · architecture

**In one line:** DiffuSpace releases Clover-35B-A3B-Preview, an Apache-licensed MoE diffusion LM using block diffusion with bidirectional Gated DeltaNet

**What:** Open-weight diffusion language model (35B total, 3B active MoE) with a thinking mode. No training data, compute or recipe is disclosed, and the page does not say whether the model was converted from a Qwen autoregressive checkpoint. Labelled Preview.

### 5. [Low-Bit GDN Recurrent-State Quantization: Global Gradient-aware Allocation and Pure-Write Sliding Windows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39931)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2610.39931
_Qinghang Huang, Xuchu Huang, Xunzhao Yin_

**In one line:** 4-bit Gated DeltaNet state quantization for Qwen3.5-4B/9B: gradient-aware per-head bits plus an FP8 window of recent writes

**What:** Quantizing the fixed-size recurrent state of linear attention saves per-request memory, but errors propagate through the recurrence and cause degraded long reasoning and repetitive, overlong outputs. GGA assigns per-head bit widths under a model-wide budget using quantization residuals weighted by gradient sensitivity to future loss. Pure-Write sliding window (PW) keeps recent writes as FP8 vector pairs that go through the full GDN transition and are merged into the low-bit historical state only after leaving the window.

**Result:** On Qwen3.5-4B/9B across seven tasks at 4 bits: GGA alone beats STEPQuant@4 by 19.48/29.84 points; GGA+PW-S8 reaches macro-averages of 69.83%/78.63%, 38.39/41.21 points above STEPQuant@4, and cuts mean generation length by a further 32.32%/36.77%.

**Critique:** Only one quantization baseline (STEPQuant) named in the abstract; the full-precision reference score, memory savings in bytes and any latency/throughput numbers were not visible (abstract only, full text rate-limited). Two models from one family; no code link.

### 6. [Look Only When Needed: Certificate-Guided Visual Routing for Efficient Diffusion VLMs](https://hyeon9698.github.io/LOWN/)

HF Daily Papers / project page · paper · inference · arXiv:2610.36671
_Donghyeon Cho, Joowon Kim, Seungho Shin, Eunho Yang_

**In one line:** LOWN skips the image in diffusion-VLM denoising steps unless a text-only prediction disagrees; ~2.8x fewer decoding FLOPs on LLaDA-V

**What:** Diffusion VLMs reprocess the full input, 89–97% of which is visual tokens, at every denoising step. LOWN first predicts masked tokens without the image and runs an image-conditioned pass only when that prediction disagrees with the last image-conditioned one; agreement certifies reuse, and the number of agreeing tokens sets how many are unmasked that step. Training-free, no tuned threshold.

**Result:** Across ten benchmarks, keeps 99.6% of LLaDA-V's score at 35.3% of decoding FLOPs (~2.8x reduction); the authors say other speed-ups at matched compute keep 92–94%, comparing against RedVTP, ZOO-Prune and KLASS.

**Critique:** Gains reported as FLOPs; wall-clock evidence on the page is a single demo (<3 s vs ~10 s). Only LLaDA-V is named explicitly; per-benchmark deltas against baselines are in figures. Applies to diffusion VLMs, which remain a niche relative to autoregressive VLMs.

### 7. [AB-Attention: Accurate Block Attention for Accelerating Video Diffusion Transformers](https://github.com/InfRec-Cardinal/AB-Attention)

HF Daily Papers / GitHub · paper · inference · arXiv:2610.39663
_Bin Xu, Mo Zhang, Zhengfeiyang Zhang, Yixing Ma, Xinfeng Yang, Wei Wang et al._

**In one line:** AB-Attention skips 89–92% of attention in video DiTs via in-block query clustering and importance-sampled KV blocks; up to 2.23x speedup

**What:** Sparse attention for video diffusion Transformers. An intra-block clustering selector scores small key/value blocks using query representatives formed within each query block; blocks are then sampled without replacement and reweighted by inclusion probability in the attention normalization, and selected blocks are gathered into full tiles executed with FlashInfer. Hopper-only CUDA kernels (sm_90a, BF16, head dim 128).

**Result:** Denoising speedup vs dense FlashAttention-3 on H20: 2.01x (Wan2.1-T2V 1.3B), 1.84x (Wan2.1 14B), 2.23x (HunyuanVideo 13B) at 89.16%/92.35% attention skipped; PSNR vs dense reference 30.8–34.4 dB. The summary reports up to 4.4 dB PSNR improvement over prior sparse methods.

**Critique:** Fidelity is measured against the dense model's own output (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS), not human or VBench-style quality; the named comparisons on the repo are DFSAttn and Sparse-VideoGen 2, and the full baseline table was not read. Speedups are on H20 only and the kernels require Hopper.

### 8. [KV Cache Quantization for Diffusion Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.799/)

ACL Anthology (EMNLP 2026 Findings) / arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2610.39853
_Yan Tai, Yunan Ding, Guangzhao Li, Siqi Luo, Chaoyang Zhang, Guangtao Zhai et al._

**In one line:** KV-cache quantization for diffusion LMs under Fast-dLLM cache reuse: TraceKV calibration and KProtect mixed-precision K cache

**What:** In diffusion LMs, Fast-dLLM-style inference reuses KV states across denoising steps even though bidirectional representations shift as tokens are revealed, so the cache is already approximate before quantization. The paper adapts KIVI and KVQuant to prefix and dual cache reuse and proposes TraceKV (reconstructs feasible reveal trajectories from prompt-answer pairs to build calibration states) and KProtect (keeps selected per-layer K-cache entries in high precision using output- and attention-level metrics, standard low-bit V).

**Result:** On LLaDA and Dream over GSM8K, HumanEval, MATH and MBPP, KProtect has the highest average score among quantized methods in the 4-bit and 2-bit prefix- and dual-cache settings (vs adapted KIVI and KVQuant). Exact scores were not available on the page read.

**Critique:** Numeric accuracy, the full-precision gap, memory savings and latency could not be verified (abstract only). Baselines are autoregressive methods adapted by the authors. Two dLLM families; no code link on the anthology page. Announced on arXiv as 2610.39853 in the 30 Oct batch.

### 9. [DolphinRouting: Cluster-Level Binary Routing for Disaggregated LLM Inference Serving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40292)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2610.40292
_Yishuo Yuan, Shaoyu Yang, Chunrong Fang_

**In one line:** DolphinRouting picks local vs remote prefill per request cluster in P/D-disaggregated serving; 5.7–7.9% lower P90 TTFT than Dynamo

**What:** In prefill-decode disaggregated serving, each request can be prefilled locally or remotely. DolphinRouting clusters requests on prompt length, cache-adjusted input length for both worker pools and output-length information, assigns one binary action per cluster via offline policy search driven by end-to-end SLO feedback, and at serving time does a feature extraction plus table lookup.

**Result:** On a mixed workload and real data on a real P/D cluster, the k-means variant cuts P90 TTFT by 5.7–7.9% and P90 TPOT by 2.6–10.1% relative to NVIDIA Dynamo.

**Critique:** Modest gains against a single named baseline; results on the three public benchmarks are not broken out in the abstract. Fixed P/D resource allocation is assumed, and the offline policy may need re-search when workloads drift. Cluster size, model and hardware details were not visible; no code link.

### 10. [Intrinsic Distribution Matching](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39633)

HF Daily Papers · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2610.39633
_Deyuan Liu, Yansen Han, Kaiwen Zheng, Jun Xie, Jiacheng Liu, Zhenglin Cheng et al._

**In one line:** IDM adds moment matching from the generator's own EMA features to DMD distillation for 1–2-step text-to-image models

**What:** Distribution matching distillation (DMD) turns diffusion models into few-step generators but struggles with detailed prompts such as long rendered text. IDM decomposes the reverse-KL field into a Gaussian moment part and a non-Gaussian residual and adds explicit constraints matching feature means and per-coordinate standard deviations, computed with the generator's own EMA copy (no external encoder), while keeping the full DMD correction. Applied with full-parameter and LoRA tuning to Z-Image-Turbo (6B) and Qwen-Image (20B). Top paper on HF Daily Papers for 30 Oct.

**Result:** At 2 NFE: 94.25% on Chinese LongText-Bench and 90.20% on English LongText-Bench, reported as the highest among compared methods at that budget; generator-EMA features perform competitively with an external DINOv2 encoder in ablations.

**Critique:** The compared baselines are not named on the abstract page and the tables were not read. Focuses on text rendering benchmarks; broader image-quality and diversity metrics are not stated. Model weights are listed as 'coming soon' in the official repo (inference code only).
