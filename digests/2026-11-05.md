### 1. [moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro (open weights)](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro)

Hugging Face model card · release · architecture

**In one line:** Kimi K3.1 Pro weights on HF: 2.8T-total/104B-active MoE, 69 KDA + 24 Gated DSA layers, MXFP4 weights via QAT, 1M context

**What:** Material update to the 26 Oct item: the weights are now public. Moonshot's blog had said they would be released by 4 Nov, and the HN front page and community posts report them going up on 4 Nov. Per the model card, the architecture is a 93-layer MoE (1 dense layer) with 896 experts, 16 routed plus 2 shared per token, and a latent MoE dimension of 3584. Attention is hybrid: 69 Kimi Delta Attention (linear) layers and 24 Gated DSA layers. Gated DSA replaces the full-attention Gated MLA layers used in Kimi K3, with an indexer of 32 heads x 128 dims and top-k 2048. It uses a SiTU-GLU activation and a 401M MoonViT-V2 vision encoder. Weights are MXFP4 and activations MXFP8, from quantization-aware training. The card recommends vLLM, SGLang and TokenSpeed. Moonshot's blog describes K3.1 Pro as a post-trained version of K3.

**Result:** Self-reported on the card: Terminal-Bench 4.0 65.7 (Claude Opus 5.5 66.4, GPT-6 Astra 58.2, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 42.3); DeepSWE v1.1 75.5 (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 77.0); GPQA-Diamond 94.2; HLE-Full 51.1 (Opus 5.5 59.8). It trails the closed models on Program Bench (31.5 vs 65.0 for Opus 5.5) and HLE-Diamond.

**Critique:** All benchmark numbers are Moonshot's own, and the card says some benchmarks are internal or use different harnesses. The card publishes no ablations for the switch from Gated MLA to Gated DSA, and no throughput or memory numbers for the long-context gains it implies. The license is a custom 'Kimi K3.1 Pro License'. Community posts say it restricts large-scale MaaS use, but this run could not confirm the terms or threshold from the card. At about 1.56 TB in MXFP4 (per secondary coverage), self-hosting needs a multi-GPU node with very large HBM.

### 2. [Multi-layer Indexer: Overlapping Selection and Attention in DeepSeek Sparse Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03768)

arXiv · paper · inference

**In one line:** Multi-layer Indexer reuses the previous layer's DSA token selection so indexer and attention overlap; no retraining needed

**What:** In DeepSeek Sparse Attention, each layer's lightning indexer scores the whole KV cache and keeps the top 2048 tokens. At long context, this scan dominates decode time and sits on the critical path before attention. MLI has each layer attend to the selection made by the previous indexer layer, so the current indexer can run concurrently with the layer's attention. It needs no retraining or new weights, composes with IndexCache (which skips indexers on some layers), runs unmodified in SGLang's decode graph, and has an optional fused decode kernel for DeepSeek-V3.2.

**Result:** Against IndexCache at equal indexer retention: up to 14.5% faster decode on DeepSeek-V3.2 and 9.8% on GLM-5 at 1/2 retention, and 7.9% / 5.1% faster at 1/4. At 128K context with 1/4 retention: 1.32x the unmodified model's decode throughput, versus 1.28x for IndexCache at 1/8. LongBench, RULER and LongBench v2 stay within a few points of IndexCache.

**Critique:** Accuracy is reported as 'within a few points', which hides the size of the loss on retrieval-heavy tasks. Using a stale selection by one layer is a lossy approximation, and the abstract gives no per-task breakdown. Speedups are relative to IndexCache, not to a full-indexer baseline at every setting, and the hardware, batch sizes and prefill effects are not given in the abstract. Results come from two DSA models only. Read via the abstract page (summary only); the paper was not read in full.

### 3. [Dory1.0-4B: A Looped Normalized-GPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03998)

arXiv · paper · architecture

**In one line:** Dory1.0-4B: looped nGPT, 6T tokens (first 4.5T in NVFP4), loop count set at inference; KV cache equals a non-looped model's

**What:** A 4B latent-recurrent language model on a normalized-Transformer (nGPT) backbone. A shared recurrent block runs a variable number of times, so one checkpoint serves several compute budgets. Per the NVIDIA model card, the layout is an 8-layer input block, a 20-layer shared recurrent block with the input reinjected on each loop, and an 8-layer output block. Sliding-window and full attention are interleaved, and 1 to 4 loops are selectable at inference. Pretraining used 6T tokens, the first ~4.5T in NVFP4. Context was then extended to 256K, followed by SFT and RLVR with a difficulty-aware loop curriculum. Only the last loop's KV is cached. BF16, MXFP8, NVFP4 and GGUF checkpoints are on Hugging Face under the OpenMDW license.

**Result:** Abstract: with 2 or more loops, it beats Qwen3.5-9B, Gemma 4 12B and Gemma 4 26B-A4B on average across 12 benchmarks. The model card's table (NVIDIA's harness) gives SWE-bench Verified of 50.3 / 59.5 / 61.8 at 1 / 2 / 4 loops, versus 61 for Qwen3.5-9B, and AA-LCR of 63 / 71 / 73, versus 68.4. GPQA Diamond is 72.4 at 2 loops, below Qwen3.5-4B's 77.7.

**Critique:** The '12-benchmark average' hides mixed results. On the card, Dory trails Qwen3.5-4B, a same-size non-looped model, on GPQA, HLE and AA-Omniscience at every loop count, which suggests looping helps agentic and long-context tasks more than knowledge. Wall-clock latency per loop is not reported in the abstract. Caching only the last loop's KV saves memory, but the effect on quality is not ablated there. Baselines were measured by NVIDIA, not independently. vLLM support is still an unmerged PR, per the card. The card lists GA as 16 Oct, earlier than the 2 Nov arXiv submission.

### 4. [FlagRot: Selective Rotation for Low-Bit KV Caches](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03992)

arXiv · paper · efficiency

**In one line:** FlagRot stores each 32-channel key block rotated or unrotated, whichever has lower attention error, for 2.5-bit KV caches

**What:** Rotating keys before quantizing (as in RotateKV/QuaRot-style methods) does not always reduce error. FlagRot decides, for each 32-channel key block, whether to store it rotated or unrotated, based on calibrated attention error, and rotates the query at read time to match. For per-channel caches such as KIVI, this is one decision per head. For per-token caches, it decides per key and stores the flag in existing sign bits, so it costs no extra memory. It ships as a vLLM plugin.

**Result:** At 2.5 bits per value, the 12-model average KL divergence for KIVI falls from 18.7 to 12.4 (x10^-3), and FlagRot beats RotateKV on 9 of 12 models. RULER at 128K improves by 2.9 points. LongBench v1 mean improves by 0.4 to 1.9 points on three of the four models where 2-bit KIVI loses accuracy. It also lowers error for 4-bit INT and FP4 caches.

**Critique:** The gains are small in absolute terms: LongBench +0.4 to 1.9 on only a subset of models. RotateKV still wins on 3 of 12 models. The abstract reports no latency or throughput cost for the read-time query rotation and flag handling. It is unclear how well the calibration transfers across domains. Rests on the arXiv RSS abstract; the abstract page was rate-limited.

### 5. [LoopKV: Loop-Aware KV Cache Compression for Serving Looped Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04137)

arXiv · paper · inference

**In one line:** LoopKV assigns each (loop, layer) KV cell 4-bit, 2-bit or reuse; 35% less KV than KIVI-4 on Ouro-1.4B in vLLM

**What:** Looped transformers multiply KV-cache size by the loop count. LoopKV profiles a model once offline and gives each (loop, layer) cell one of three treatments: 4-bit storage, 2-bit storage, or reuse of the final loop's KV. A planner minimizes memory reads under damage and storage budgets. A single fused kernel in vLLM, run under CUDA graphs, executes the mixed plan.

**Result:** On Ouro-1.4B on one RTX PRO 6000, the memory-optimized LoopKV-M plan stores 35% less KV than uniform 4-bit (KIVI-4) with the same fused kernel. Under a latency SLO at 16K max length, it sustains 1.7-2.2x KIVI-4's goodput. Averages on six benchmarks are not significantly below BF16.

**Critique:** It is evaluated on a single small looped model (1.4B) on one GPU at 16K context. Whether it carries over to larger looped models such as Dory-4B, or to long contexts, is untested. The only baseline is uniform KIVI-4, with no comparison to newer mixed-precision or eviction methods. 'Not significantly below BF16' on six benchmarks gives no effect sizes. Rests on the arXiv RSS abstract.

### 6. [ARLoop: Fully-Open Pre-trained Looped Transformers with Attention Residuals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05234)

arXiv · paper · architecture

**In one line:** ARLoop replaces the residual stream in looped transformers with attention over depth; open 1.2B model on 0.5T tokens

**What:** In looped transformers, each layer's input is normally the running residual sum. ARLoop instead aggregates earlier intermediate representations selectively, using attention over depth. The authors find that sliding-window depth attention matches or beats attention over the full depth history at larger recurrence depths, which bounds its cost. Models are released fully open.

**Result:** At 140M and 370M parameters with recurrence depths R=1-5, it beats prior residual designs for looped models. A 1.2B model trained on 0.5T tokens with three recurrences outperforms Huginn-3.5B on all evaluated tasks, with the largest gains on math and coding, and is competitive with Qwen3.5-2B.

**Critique:** Huginn-3.5B is a weak, older baseline. 'Competitive with Qwen3.5-2B' is not a win, and Qwen trained on far more data. The abstract does not report the compute or memory overhead of depth attention per loop step, which matters for inference cost. The scale is small (≤1.2B). Rests on the arXiv RSS abstract.

### 7. [Near-Zero Token Embeddings Silently Stall Training in Gated Sequence Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04449)

HF Daily Papers · paper · training

**In one line:** Near-zero input embeddings in untied Mamba/Gated DeltaNet/xLSTM models blow up gradients; GroupClip and GateFloor proposed

**What:** Rare or template tokens get no input gradient while AdamW weight decay shrinks them toward zero. In gated sequence models (Mamba, Gated DeltaNet, xLSTM), a zero input makes the gates vanish. That suppresses readout from the state, collapses residual norms, and lets Pre-RMSNorm amplify backward gradients layer by layer. FP32 global-norm clipping can then overflow and zero out the whole batch's update. Transformers are protected by non-initial positions or a BOS token. Proposed fixes: GroupClip clips input-embedding gradients separately, for already-released checkpoints. GateFloor adds a small positive offset to the gate, for use in pretraining.

**Result:** About half of the untied gated or hybrid checkpoints among 291 surveyed open-weight repos had embedding rows with norm below 1e-6, while no tied models did. In Telugu continual pretraining of Nemotron-3-Nano-4B, about 20% of update steps were discarded. GroupClip eliminated the lost updates in their tests. GateFloor greatly reduced near-zero gradients with comparable perplexity in models up to 370M.

**Critique:** GateFloor is validated only up to 370M parameters, so its effect at production scale is unknown. The failure is shown on one continual-pretraining case (Telugu, a low-resource language with many unseen tokens), which may overstate how often it happens in ordinary pretraining. Perplexity is called 'comparable', with no downstream evals. No code link is listed. Rests on the Hugging Face paper page.

### 8. [P²-MOPD: Accelerating Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation via Parameter Priors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03953)

arXiv · paper · training

**In one line:** P²-MOPD learns student/expert merge weights during on-policy distillation; 94.7% vs 80.9% recovery for MOPD at 1.7B

**What:** Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) trains a student against several domain experts. P²-MOPD treats a linear combination of student and expert parameters as one differentiable model. It jointly optimizes the mixing coefficients and the student weights under the same distillation objective, so the experts act as parameter priors while the student stays trainable. This differs from task arithmetic, which is a training-free merge confined to the span of the frozen experts.

**Result:** Across four domains at 1.7B and 4B, it reaches MOPD's final average recovery in half the training steps. At 1.7B, average recovery is 94.7% versus 80.9% for MOPD.

**Critique:** It assumes the experts share the student's architecture and initialization, which limits where it applies. The abstract gives only the 1.7B headline number, so the 4B gains are unstated. 'Recovery' is relative to the experts, not to absolute benchmark scores. Compute per step (forwarding the merged model) is not compared, so 'half the steps' may not mean half the cost. Rests on the arXiv RSS abstract.

### 9. [Mixture of Wide Experts: Sparse Routing for Interpretable Native Coordinates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03655)

arXiv · paper · architecture

**In one line:** MoWE: sparse FFN whose experts write to fixed disjoint output coordinates; more interpretable, higher loss than standard MoE

**What:** A sparse feed-forward layer in which each selected expert reads the full residual representation but writes only to its own fixed, disjoint set of output coordinates. The goal is residual-stream coordinates that are interpretable natively, without training a sparse autoencoder afterwards.

**Result:** In 19-layer models trained on 10B tokens at roughly matched compute: eval cross-entropy is 2.417 for MoWE, 2.452 for dense and 2.22 for standard MoE. Specificity scores are 0.523 for MoWE, 0.375 for dense and 0.268 for MoE. TopK SAEs on the FFN updates score 0.489 (dense) and 0.556 (MoWE).

**Critique:** Standard MoE is clearly better on loss (2.22 vs 2.417), so this trades a lot of modeling quality for interpretability. Two runs per architecture at small scale and 10B tokens is too little to judge scaling. The specificity metric is the authors' own, and the steering case studies are anecdotal. Rests on the arXiv RSS abstract.

### 10. [Length Is Cheap, Competition Is Not: Attention Predicts Context Rot in Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04169)

arXiv · paper · other

**In one line:** Long-context retrieval loss tracks competing statements, not length: attention mass on evidence explains up to 58% of loss variance

**What:** Long-context retrieval probes on five open models (0.5B-3B) compare three kinds of padding: neutral filler, same-format distractor statements, and statements that give the queried key a rival value. The study then relates retrieval loss to the attention mass left on the true evidence.

**Result:** With neutral filler, Qwen2.5-1.5B keeps 0.97-1.00 accuracy up to 32K tokens. Same-format statements drop accuracy to 0.16-0.33, and a single rival value multiplies loss by 9-110x. Across 126,473 trials, attention mass explains up to 58% of loss variance, against at most 8% for log length. A masked-filler protocol used in earlier work loses 11-18 MMLU points at 30K tokens.

**Critique:** Single-author study on small models (≤3B) and synthetic key-value retrieval. Whether it holds for frontier long-context models, or for hybrid and sparse-attention architectures, is untested. It shows correlation with attention mass but proposes no mitigation. Rests on the arXiv RSS abstract.
