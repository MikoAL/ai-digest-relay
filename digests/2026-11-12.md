### 1. [ADIndex: Layer-Adaptive Sparse Attention Indexing via Group-Level Distillation](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.12956)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.12956
_Jin-Fei Qi, Yiduo Li, Jiang-Xin Shi, Kan Liu, Tao Lan, Hanlin Tang et al._

**In one line:** ADIndex speeds up the DeepSeek Sparse Attention indexer: up to 6.22x prefill and 3.40x decode kernel speedup at 256K context

**What:** In DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) the lightweight indexer still scores the entire causal prefix for every query, and at long context it can take over half of prefill time. ADIndex makes the indexer layer-adaptive: layer-wise calibration decides per-layer indexer capacity, smaller indexers are trained by group-level distillation from the original, and queries in a group share a token bank so token selection is not repeated per query.

**Result:** Up to 6.22x prefill and 3.40x decode kernel speedups at 256K context and about 1.54x end-to-end serving speedup at 128K, described as near-lossless on LongBench, LongBench v2 and RULER versus the original DSA indexer.

**Critique:** Rests on the arXiv cs.LG listing-page abstract only; the abstract page could not be fetched this run. Kernel speedups are against the stock DSA indexer only; no comparison to other sparse-attention or indexer-pruning methods (e.g. LISA-style indexer fusion) is visible. 'Near-lossless' is not quantified in the listing summary, and the model(s) and hardware used were not stated there. Distillation cost per layer is not reported in what was read.

### 2. [Looping Deeper Without Growing the KV Cache: Recurrent Depth for Causal Encoder-Decoders](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.13378)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.13378
_Jiaqi Leng, Minshen Zhang, Zhaofeng Sun, Yu-Yang Qian, Peng Zhao, Hao Zhang_

**In one line:** Looped causal encoder-decoder keeps 86-93% of a looped Transformer's gain with 1/6 of its global KV; 3.6x throughput at 552B

**What:** Looped (recurrent-depth) Transformers grow the KV cache with every loop round. This paper splits the model into a causal encoder and decoder and puts the recurrence only in the encoder, where repeated rounds refine a global KV representation; training with a stochastic number of rounds makes each round reuse the previous round's cache, so the KV footprint stays fixed regardless of depth.

**Result:** Recovers 86-93% of a standard looped Transformer's quality gain while using one-sixth of its global KV. On a 552B-parameter model looped 8 rounds, it holds 36.8x less KV per token and serves 3.6x higher throughput on 8K-token prompts.

**Critique:** Rests on the arXiv cs.LG listing-page abstract only; the abstract page could not be fetched this run. The 36.8x and 3.6x figures are against a looped Transformer baseline, not against a non-looped model of equal compute, so the cost/benefit versus simply using a larger dense model is unclear from the abstract. Which benchmarks define the 'gain' and the training compute for the 552B run are not stated in what was read.

### 3. [SinkState: On-the-Fly SVD of Recurrent States for Efficient Hybrid LLM Serving](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.14246)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.14246
_Haolin Jiang, Weihang Li, Zhongzhu Zhou, Jinwen Pan, Santosh Pandey, Hang Liu_

**In one line:** SinkState stores linear-attention recurrent states in low-rank form: up to 2.6x less state memory, 1.58x faster decode on hybrid LLMs

**What:** In hybrid models (Mamba-2 / Gated DeltaNet plus attention), recurrent states are stored densely, so their memory traffic grows with batch size and dominates decode. The authors observe that Q/K/V of linear-attention layers concentrate in a stable low-dimensional subspace (a 'linear attention sink'), making each state effectively low-rank with varying rank. SinkState keeps states in adaptive low-rank form via buffered updates, a local SVD restricted to the top subspace, and asynchronous CPU consolidation of the remaining components.

**Result:** On four hybrid models from 4B to 120B parameters, peak recurrent-state memory drops by up to 2.6x (1.9x average) and decoding is up to 1.58x faster (1.25x average) than Mamba-SSM and FLA kernels, with accuracy reported as maintained.

**Critique:** Rests on the arXiv cs.LG listing-page abstract only; the abstract page could not be fetched this run. Accuracy preservation is asserted without numbers in the listing summary; low-rank truncation could hurt long-range recall tasks that are not mentioned. Baselines are dense-state kernels, not other state-compression schemes. CPU consolidation adds host dependence whose cost at high concurrency is not described in what was read.

### 5. [Decode Token Parallelism: Memory-Efficient Distributed Attention Parallelism for Scalable LLM Decoding](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.13293)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.13293
_Susav Shrestha, Brad Settlemyer, Narasimha Reddy_

**In one line:** Decode Token Parallelism in vLLM: up to 1.7x decode throughput over DCP and 2.06x over data parallelism on Llama 3.1 405B

**What:** Distributed decoding usually ties attention to how weights are sharded, so KV caches get split or replicated. Decode Token Parallelism decouples weight placement from attention ownership: each node runs attention only over its own requests' local KV cache, while the weight-parallel parts of the layer are shared across the decode pool.

**Result:** Implemented in vLLM and tested on Llama 3.1 405B and Nemotron-4 340B with up to 64 H100s: up to 1.7x throughput over decode context parallelism (DCP) and up to 2.06x over data parallelism at matched GPU count.

**Critique:** Rests on the arXiv cs.LG listing-page abstract only; the abstract page could not be fetched this run. Evaluated on dense models only, on H100s; MoE models and newer hardware are not covered in what was read. 'Up to' figures hide how gains vary with sequence length and batch mix; load imbalance when requests have very different KV lengths is a plausible weakness not addressed in the summary.

### 6. [Multi-Future Drafting: Mixture Block Drafters for Tree-Verified Speculative Decoding](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.13017)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.13017
_Haoyu Huang, Jialiang Kang, Linlin Yang, Xiao Yang, Baochang Zhang_

**In one line:** Mixture block drafter proposes several coherent futures per pass for tree speculative decoding: up to 7.8x speedup on Qwen targets

**What:** Block drafters that predict several tokens at once, when expanded into a verification tree, often produce 'chimeric' prefixes that mix tokens from different plausible continuations and get rejected. This drafter learns K mode embeddings so that one forward pass yields K internally consistent candidate futures, which are then tree-verified.

**Result:** On four Qwen targets from 4B to 27B at batch size 1, up to 7.8x speedup; 5-8% faster than the best single-mode tree and 9-16% faster than DARTree on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B.

**Critique:** Rests on the arXiv cs.LG listing-page abstract only; the abstract page could not be fetched this run. Results are at batch size 1, where speculative decoding helps most; behaviour at serving batch sizes is not reported in what was read. Only Qwen-family targets are tested, and the margin over the strongest baselines (5-16%) is much smaller than the headline 7.8x over autoregressive decoding.

### 8. [Train High, Ship Low: Efficient LoRA Synchronization for Distributed On-Policy Post-Training](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.13306)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.13306
_Shenghui Li, Li Ju, Thiemo Voigt_

**In one line:** HiLo ships compressed LoRA gradients instead of adapters to rollout workers: as little as 1.6% of bytes, up to 2.2x faster updates

**What:** In distributed on-policy RL post-training, updated LoRA adapters must be shipped to rollout workers each step, which bottlenecks on bandwidth. HiLo instead sends compressed gradients and lets workers replay the update through replicated optimizer states; its Spar compressor combines spectral truncation, adaptive rank allocation and error feedback.

**Result:** Learning quality close to full synchronization while transmitting as little as 1.6% of adapter bytes, with end-to-end updates up to 2.2x faster under bandwidth limits.

**Critique:** Rests on the arXiv cs.LG listing-page abstract only; the abstract page could not be fetched this run. Speedups apply only in bandwidth-constrained settings; in well-connected clusters adapter transfer is rarely the bottleneck. Replicating optimizer state on every worker adds memory not quantified in what was read. Model sizes, tasks and the precise quality gap to full sync are not stated in the listing summary.

### 9. [Closing the BF16-FP16 Convergence Speed Gap in RL Post-Training with Stochastic Rounding](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.13545)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.13545
_Mahdi Nazemi, Oleg Rybakov, Tijmen Blankevoort, Asit Mishra, Carlo del Mundo_

**In one line:** Stochastic rounding of FP32 shadow weights lets BF16 RL post-training converge as fast as FP16 without loss scaling

**What:** In RL with verifiable rewards, BF16 mixed precision converges more slowly than FP16 at the same learning rate. The authors attribute this to 'weight sticking': round-to-nearest conversion of FP32 shadow weights to BF16 drops small updates. Raising the learning rate compensates but increases KL from the reference policy; they instead apply stochastic rounding when quantizing the shadow weights.

**Result:** Matches FP16's convergence speed and gives lower KL divergence than BF16 with a raised learning rate, with no loss scaling needed. The listing abstract gives no specific numbers.

**Critique:** Rests on the arXiv cs.LG listing-page abstract only; the abstract page could not be fetched this run. No quantitative results (models, tasks, step counts, KL values) were visible, so the size of the effect cannot be judged. Stochastic rounding is a known technique for low-precision training; the novelty is the diagnosis in the RL setting. The overhead of stochastic rounding in the optimizer step is not reported in what was read.

### 10. [Placing LLM Lookup Tables by Per-Layer Prefetch Deadlines](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.13413)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.13413
_Wen Huang, Jack Liu_

**In one line:** SlackTier places DeepSeek-V4.1's 203 GB lookup tables on NVMe by per-layer deadlines: host memory 212 to 34 GiB, decode at most +1.4%

**What:** DeepSeek-V4.1's n-gram (Engram) lookup tables occupy most host memory in a 4-GPU serving process. Since row indices depend only on input tokens, reads can start at each step and overlap earlier layers, with very different slack per layer. The authors model how this deadline, session reuse and device contention decide which storage tier each table can live in, and build SlackTier, a table tier for SGLang.

**Result:** With tables on four NVMe disks, mean time per output token rises at most 1.4% over unmodified SGLang while host memory falls from 212 to 34 GiB; reusing the freed memory as host KV cache raises median throughput by 36% on a KV-constrained agent workload.

**Critique:** Rests on the arXiv cs.LG listing-page abstract only; the abstract page could not be fetched this run. Specific to models with large token-indexed lookup tables (DeepSeek-V4.1 Engram); results on agent and chat replays may not cover worst-case cache-miss patterns. Requires four NVMe disks per process; the 36% gain depends on a workload chosen to be KV-constrained.

### 11. [Beyond the Log-Linear Frontier: Pre-Asymptotic Analysis of Finite-Budget Neural Scaling Laws](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.13534)

arXiv · paper · scaling · arXiv:2611.13534
_Tianyu Zhao, Zhixiang Ren_

**In one line:** Pre-asymptotic scaling-law analysis fits Pythia, Chinchilla and RegMix runs with 5.89% holdout MAPE and lowers allocation regret

**What:** Empirical learning curves and compute-optimal allocations deviate from the separable power law at finite budgets. The paper attributes this to spectral and domain heterogeneity and models it analytically: Mellin-Barnes analysis of covariance resolvents plus Aitken differencing along the model-capacity axis, and KKT duality with water-filling for data/domain mixing.

**Result:** 5.89% holdout MAPE across 45 operator spectra, Pythia (70M-12B), Chinchilla (245 runs) and RegMix (17 domains); up to 76.4% lower high-compute allocation regret and 59.4% lower cross-scale mixture RMSE.

**Critique:** Rests on the arXiv cs.LG listing-page abstract only; the abstract page could not be fetched this run. The baselines behind the regret and RMSE reductions are not named in the listing abstract, so the comparison cannot be assessed. Fits are on existing public runs; no new compute-optimal training run validates the predicted allocations prospectively.
