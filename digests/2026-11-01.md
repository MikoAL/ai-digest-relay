### 1. [Multi-View MoE: Letting Every Router Shape Expert Computation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40577)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2610.40577
_Zelin Zhao, Tianchi Ma, Junyuan Shang, Shuohuan Wang, Dianhai Yu_

**In one line:** Multi-View MoE: several sigmoid routers pick experts and blend latent input views, same active experts/FLOPs; +1.18 pts on a 10B-A1B model

**What:** The authors densely evaluate all experts and find that a single router's ranking agrees only weakly with measured expert utility, especially in middle and deep layers. Multi-View MoE keeps the active-expert budget fixed but (1) Multi-Router: combines sigmoid scores from several independently initialized routers, which learn distinct preferences, so an expert can be picked because one router strongly favours it or several jointly support it; (2) Multi-View: uses each selected expert's relative scores across routers to blend learned latent views into that expert's input. Each selected expert still runs one forward pass, so active experts, expert FLOPs and all-to-all volume are unchanged.

**Result:** 24-layer 10B-A1B MoE trained on 708B tokens: full Multi-View MoE improves the nine-task downstream average by 1.18 points over the baseline MoE; Multi-Router alone gives +0.6 points and raises router-score vs expert-utility rank correlation by 8.1% globally and ~58% in the worst-aligned layer.

**Critique:** Read from the arXiv abstract only. One model scale and one baseline (the authors' own standard router); no comparison against other routing improvements (e.g. expert-choice, auxiliary-loss-free balancing) is mentioned. Router and view-mixing parameters add overhead that the abstract does not quantify, and wall-clock training/inference cost is not reported. Gains of ~1 point on a nine-task average need variance estimates. No code link on the abstract page.

### 2. [GAAC: Lossless KV Compression for Bandwidth-Efficient LLM Inference on Personal Devices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38210)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2610.38210
_Yipin Guo, Arun M George, Siddharth Joshi_

**In one line:** GAAC: bit-exact BF16 KV-cache compression (~1.41x) from exponent codes and a fused attention kernel; 1.19-1.27x decode attention at 64K

**What:** Targets KV-cache memory bandwidth on personal devices (DGX Spark, Apple M4) without any approximation. Group-Adaptive Additive Codec encodes BF16 exponents with calibrated per-channel base-plus-offset codes over contiguous ranges and adapts code widths across small groups, keeping KV values bit-for-bit identical. A fused attention kernel reconstructs KV tiles on the fly instead of decompressing the whole cache; block-wise compression with fixed-capacity slots supports incremental appends and paged serving.

**Result:** Lossless compression 1.405-1.407x for BF16 KV across five models; 1.09-1.14x for FP8 E4M3 and 1.25-1.30x for E5M2 caches. On DGX Spark at 64K context: decode attention 1.19-1.27x faster, end-to-end W4A16 decode steps 1.04-1.15x faster; ~26% less resident KV memory and 13-29% higher aggregate serving throughput at comparable KV budgets. Apple M4: 1.02-1.25x attention speedup.

**Critique:** Comparisons are against uncompressed KV and the same stack without GAAC; the abstract names no competing lossless or lossy KV compressors (e.g. KV quantization, other entropy/exponent coders). End-to-end gains (1.04-1.15x) are much smaller than kernel gains. Ratio for FP8 caches is modest. Model list, batch sizes and calibration sensitivity were not visible on the abstract page; no code link listed.

### 3. [LRMoE: Demand-Side Load Re-Calibration for Efficient MoE Training on Memory-Constrained Nodes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37720)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2610.37720
_Jiaming Hu, Zhida Jiang, Zhaolong Xing, Yu Li, Zhen Chen, Ke Zhang_

**In one line:** LRMoE drops selected token-expert pairs from MoE backward passes to cut traffic; 21-45% less MoE-layer time to fixed loss vs SYMI

**What:** Uneven MoE routing creates compute/communication hot spots, and replicating hot experts competes with token buffers for GPU memory. LRMoE plans retention quotas and expert placement in the forward pass while keeping all routed forward computation. In backpropagation it uses a load-recalibration metric to choose which token-expert pairs contribute to expert weight updates, routes those under capacity constraints, and physically compacts the retained pairs so backward all-to-all payloads, compute and buffer demand shrink.

**Result:** On Qwen3-30B-A3B and GLM4.5-106B-A12B, 21.2-44.8% less cumulative MoE-layer training time to fixed-loss targets than SYMI.

**Critique:** Dropping token-expert pairs from gradient updates changes the optimisation; the abstract reports time-to-loss but no downstream quality after training, and the runs appear to be short fixed-loss targets rather than full pretraining. Speedup is for MoE layers only, not end-to-end step time. Only one baseline (SYMI) is named; no comparison with capacity-factor dropping or other load balancers. Hardware and cluster size not stated in the abstract; no code link.

### 4. [Your LR Schedule May Not Be Principled: Effective Learning Rate Scheduling in Weight-Normalized Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40362)

arXiv / HF Daily Papers · paper · training · arXiv:2610.40362
_Qifan Yu, Xinyu Ma, Xingyan Bin, Di He_

**In one line:** Paper argues weight-normalized training gains come from its effective-LR trajectory; proposes AIR schedule, ~0.01 lower loss than cosine

**What:** Weight normalization (fixed-norm weights) has been reported to beat weight decay in LLM pretraining. The authors argue the gain comes mostly from the effective learning rate (ELR) trajectory that WN induces: when ELR trajectories of WN and weight decay are matched, loss curves largely coincide. They propose designing schedules in ELR space and introduce AIR (Affine Inverse-square-Root decay): a 1/sqrt(t) decay with an affine correction so it hits a chosen peak and terminal LR over a finite horizon. They also find late-stage ELR scheduling is the main source of gains and reduces sensitivity to earlier LR choices.

**Result:** 128B-token MoE pretraining: AIR beats cosine and other candidate schedules on training loss, downstream scores and hyperparameter robustness; at matched cumulative LR, terminal training loss is ~0.01 lower than cosine. Applying the same ELR trajectory to a weight-decay run reproduces the WN dynamics.

**Critique:** Read from the arXiv abstract only. A 0.01 loss gain is small and needs seed variance to be meaningful; model size of the MoE is not given in the abstract, and only one token budget (128B) is reported, so behaviour at longer horizons or with WSD-style schedules is unknown. 'Other candidate schedules' are not named in the abstract. No code link.

### 5. [NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B)

Hugging Face model card · release · architecture

**In one line:** NVIDIA releases Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B: 40-language speech LLM on a hybrid Mamba-2/Transformer MoE with built-in MTP drafting

**What:** Open-weights (OpenMDW-1.1) offline transcription model released 28 Oct: 34B total / ~4.3B active parameters. A ~600M Transformer ASR encoder plus a ~99M speaker encoder from Nemotron-3-Diarization (a 'parallel expert encoder') feed the Nemotron-3.5 Lightning 30B-A3B LLM, a Mamba-2-Transformer hybrid MoE. It adds a CTC head for word timestamps, a native multi-token-prediction layer used as the speculative-decoding draft in vLLM, and an optional separately trained DFlash2 draft model. 65,536-token context, recordings up to one hour; BF16, FP8 and NVFP4 checkpoints.

**Result:** Model card: English Open ASR 9-set mean WER 4.71 vs 4.94 (Qwen3-ASR-1.7B); multilingual 7-language mean WER 4.62 vs 6.49 (Qwen3-ASR-1.7B); long-form WER 10.98 vs 13.37 (Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3); multi-speaker cpWER 15.80 vs 16.83 (DiCoW v3.3 + DiariZen). ~410 RTFx on one H100 in BF16 with MTP2.

**Critique:** All numbers are NVIDIA's own and dated 12 Oct; not independently reproduced. Baselines are much smaller (0.6-1.7B) models, so per-active-parameter comparison is not apples-to-apples, and no comparison against large closed ASR APIs is given. The headline 2,238 RTFx figure uses NVFP4 + DFlash2 in a NIM stack not in the open-source release. NVIDIA's research page describes the backbone only as hybrid Mamba-Transformer MoE, without saying Mamba-2.

### 6. [Read, Allocate, Route: Role-Coupled Tokenization for Pixel-Space Generative Transformers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40647)

HF Daily Papers · paper · architecture · arXiv:2610.40647
_Jiongfan Yang, Weichen Fan, Haiwen Diao, Zhongang Cai, Ziwei Liu_

**In one line:** SpecSlot: pixel-space flow Transformer sends only the lowest-DCT-band slot to global attention; 2.84 better FID at ImageNet-512

**What:** Pixel-space generators normally use one token per patch, so finer detail means longer sequences and quadratic global attention. SpecSlot (a rectified-flow Transformer) decouples output detail from attention length: each spatial anchor expands into several slots, each owning a disjoint band of DCT coefficients; only the lowest-frequency slot per anchor takes part in global attention, while all slots interact locally. A spatial head adds a local correction to the spectral prediction, and state, target and loss stay in pixel space.

**Result:** ImageNet-256: final FID comparable to a FLOPs-matched JiT control, with faster convergence. ImageNet-512: FID 2.84 better than the control with 3.9% fewer FLOPs, and within 0.69 FID of JiT-B/16 at 38.2% lower cost. Text-to-image 256x256: DPG-Bench +1.06 over JiT-B/8 (62.21 vs 61.15) at 40.6% lower forward cost (129.86 vs 218.77 GFLOPs).

**Critique:** Baselines are JiT variants only; no comparison against latent-diffusion models or other token-reduction methods for pixel-space DiTs. At ImageNet-256 there is no final-FID gain, and it still trails JiT-B/16 by 0.69 FID at 512. FLOPs, not measured wall-clock, are reported; local slot interaction may not map efficiently onto attention kernels. Image generation rather than LLMs, but the attention-length decoupling is the architecture idea of interest. Code is on GitHub.

### 7. [Broken on Arrival: Silently Defective LLM Artifacts in Public Model Registries and How to Catch Them](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05881)

arXiv / HF Daily Papers · paper · inference · arXiv:2609.05881
_Aditi Patodiya_

**In one line:** Audit of 305 official Ollama GGUF quants finds 5 (1.6%) silently score 0/164 on HumanEval+; releases quantcheck screening tool

**What:** Audits quantized GGUF (llama.cpp k-quants, ~2-8 bit) artifacts as distributed: a census of 305 code-capable instruct artifacts (<=8 GB, 15 model lines) in the official Ollama library plus 22 top community Hugging Face conversions. Screening uses a 15-task HumanEval+ smoke suite calibrated on 24 Qwen2.5-Coder configurations; suspects must then fail the full 164-task suite, fail on both CUDA and Apple Metal, and fail where an independent conversion of the same model succeeds. Also tests cheap output statistics (repetition, truncation, prompt echo) as pre-filters.

**Result:** 5/305 official artifacts (1.6%; Wilson 95% CI 0.7-3.8%) are silently defective, scoring 0/164: four Qwen2.5-Coder-3B low-bit conversions (q2_K, q3_K_S/M/L) and one phi3.5-mini q2_K; 2 of 29 conversion groups affected. 20/22 community files were healthy; two deepseek-coder-6.7B files failed on CUDA but passed on Metal. Best output-statistics rule caught 3/5 defects at 2.3% false positives. Of five day-zero community conversions of a new 30B model, four failed to load.

**Critique:** Single independent author; GGUF and coding tasks only (no AWQ/GPTQ, no non-code tasks), and the community sample is small (22). Miss-rate bound for the smoke screen is loose (0-27.8%). Root cause of the defects is not established. This is v2 of a September paper surfaced on HF Daily Papers on 30 Oct; the PDF did not show a version date. Code and data: github.com/aditi-p31/quantcheck.

### 8. [A Unified Hessian-Guided Framework for Post-Training Quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40345)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2610.40345
_Mads Toftrup, Marco Federici, Boris van Breugel, Paul Whatmough, Markus Nagel_

**In one line:** Unified PTQ: jointly optimizes quantization ranges, linear transforms and rounding under one Hessian/Fisher output-sensitivity loss

**What:** LLM post-training quantization usually tunes quantization ranges, linear (rotation/scaling) transformations and weight rounding separately, each against local layer-reconstruction error, which does not reflect how errors change final predictions. The authors derive a Hessian-guided approximation of the output change caused by joint weight and activation quantization and, via a tractable Fisher approximation, optimise ranges, transformations and rounding jointly under that single output-sensitivity objective.

**Result:** The abstract reports no numbers; it states the method consistently outperforms state-of-the-art transformation and rounding methods in both channel-wise and block-wise quantization settings. Figures are in the paper body, which could not be read in this run.

**Critique:** Only the abstract was read (arXiv rate-limited the HTML/PDF), so models, bit-widths, baselines and the size of the gains are unverified here. Fisher/Hessian-based PTQ has a long history (OBQ/GPTQ, BRECQ), so the novelty lies in joint optimisation; the calibration-data and compute cost of the joint search is not stated in the abstract.

### 9. [Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40564)

HF Daily Papers · paper · training · arXiv:2610.40564
_Yuda Song, Alvin Zhang, Fahim Tajwar, Daman Arora, Jeff Schneider, Daniel Khashabi et al._

**In one line:** CMU paper casts pretraining, SFT and RL as one likelihood-fitting algorithm and mixes static and RL-generated data in a single trainer

**What:** Argues that pretraining, midtraining, SFT and RL do not need different learning algorithms: suitable RL objectives can be written as maximum-likelihood fitting to target distributions generated by the model, so every stage becomes likelihood training on some data distribution. Training then reduces to choosing which data, in what proportions and when; conventional staged training is a special case. They build a system that mixes static data with RL-generated samples in one trainer.

**Result:** The abstract gives no numbers; it claims a better accuracy-compute trade-off and task coverage, better preservation of broad capabilities and a better starting point for continual learning across controlled and real-world experiments.

**Critique:** Quantitative results, model scales and baselines were not visible on the paper page, so the claims are unverified here. Rewriting RL as likelihood fitting to model-generated targets resembles reward-weighted regression / expert-iteration style methods; how it differs in practice from those and from simply interleaving SFT and RL data needs the full paper. Code is released (github.com/yudasong/unified-training). Off focus: training recipe rather than architecture or efficiency.
