### 1. [Supercharging Olmo-core for Efficient and Scalable MoE Training](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.18956)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.18956
_Tianhua Tao, Akshita Bhagia, Dirk Groeneveld, Pete Walsh, Tyler Romero, Yashas Samaga et al._

**In one line:** Olmo-core rebuilt for MoE: DDP + expert/pipeline parallelism, MXFP8; 858 TFLOP/s per B300 GPU at 1.2T params on up to 512 GPUs

**What:** Systems paper on the open Olmo-core training stack for Mixture-of-Experts models. It replaces FSDP with DDP plus expert parallelism and pipeline parallelism, adds NVSHMEM-based row-wise expert routing (token dispatch/combine), MXFP8 compute and per-layer activation recompute, and an optional DeepEP v2 backend. It also documents a router failure mode the authors call 'Token Gerrymandering', where the router learns to game the load-balancing loss rather than balance real load.

**Result:** On NVL8 B300 nodes the stack trains MoE models from 12.9B to 1.2T total parameters on up to 512 GPUs; at 1.2T with MXFP8 and per-layer recompute it reaches 858 useful-model TFLOP/s per GPU. An experimental capacity test with DeepEP v2 reached 2.38T total parameters.

**Critique:** Throughput is reported in the authors' own 'useful-model TFLOP/s' metric; the abstract gives no head-to-head against Megatron-Core, TorchTitan or DeepSpeed on the same hardware, and no MFU in comparable terms. The 2.38T run is a capacity test, not a training run with convergence data. Whether MXFP8 changes loss curves versus BF16 is not stated in the abstract. Cross-listed from cs.DC. Details rest on the abstract as shown in the arXiv cs.LG/new listing (17 Nov); the abstract page itself was not read.

### 2. [Latent Lattice Language Models](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.21608)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.21608
_Shivam Duggal, Zongze Wu, William T. Freeman, Antonio Torralba, Richard Zhang, Eli Shechtman_

**In one line:** Latent Lattice LMs add per-layer latent streams that persist across tokens; 406M model: val PPL 15.41 to 14.40 vs matched Transformer

**What:** Adds a second axis of state to the Transformer: alongside the usual vertical residual stream, each layer keeps a set of 'horizontal' latent streams that persist from token to token and condition that layer's attention, KV-cache writes and MLP computation. The idea is to increase the number of active computational states without adding parameters, and it is also applied on top of Gated DeltaNet (linear-attention) backbones. (HN front-page coverage on 17 Nov attributed the work to MIT and Adobe.)

**Result:** Trained on 20B FineWeb-Edu tokens, a 406M-parameter Lattice model lowers validation perplexity from 15.41 to 14.40 versus a parameter-matched Transformer; combined with hyper-connections and input residuals it reaches 13.61, versus 14.22 for a Transformer with the same enhancements. Lattice-GDN and Lattice-Triadic-GDN beat pure Gated DeltaNet baselines that had 4x larger native states.

**Critique:** Single scale (406M, 20B tokens) and perplexity only; no downstream or long-context evaluation is cited in the abstract. A cross-token latent stream adds a sequential dependency, so training parallelism, wall-clock and decode overhead are the key missing numbers; parameter-matched is not FLOP-matched. Details rest on the abstract as shown in the arXiv cs.LG/new listing (17 Nov); the abstract page itself was not read.

### 3. [Multi-Branch Transformer](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.19800)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.19800
_Gaotang Li, Lingjie Chen, Chunyuan Deng, Zhiwei Xu, Hongzheng Chen, Christopher De Sa et al._

**In one line:** Multi-Branch Transformer runs parallel block branches with a shared Joint-KV cache: up to 34% less KV, up to 51.4% higher decode throughput

**What:** Replaces part of the sequential layer stack with parallel branches: shared early layers, then several independently parameterized branches of Transformer blocks, then a fusion step and shared final layers. Three variants trade width for depth at comparable training compute (Wide-Shallow, Narrow-Deep, Balanced). Joint-KV builds one shared KV cache per depth from the branch representations, and a custom inference engine uses branch grouping and shared-cache reuse.

**Result:** In controlled pretraining of four MoE models from 0.8B to 2.1B total parameters, 2-branch Wide-Shallow MBTs beat sequential Transformers at matched parameters and training compute. Fitted scaling curves imply standard Transformers need up to 1.26x the training body compute to match the final Balanced+Joint-KV models' validation loss; full-model KV storage drops by up to 34% and decoding throughput rises up to 51.4% on one H200. Ablations put the best branch placement in the middle layers.

**Critique:** Small scale (≤2.1B total MoE params) and the gains are reported as relative maxima ('up to') without absolute losses or downstream scores in the abstract. The 1.26x figure comes from fitted curves rather than a direct large-scale run. Throughput depends on the authors' own engine; comparison to standard serving stacks (vLLM/SGLang) and to other KV-sharing methods (e.g. cross-layer KV sharing, MLA) is not stated.

### 4. [Gradient-Guided Hardware-Native MIXFP4](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.18769)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.18769
_Jian-Jia Chen, Brian J. Chan, Wen-Hung Lee, Yi-Ting Li, Po-Shen Chen, Kuan-Te Kuo et al._

**In one line:** FlipQuant picks E2M1 or E0M3 per NVFP4 weight tile via KL gradients; closes 42.5% of the PPL gap to BF16 vs 13.3% for FourOverSix

**What:** Targets the remaining perplexity gap of NVFP4 W4A4 inference on Blackwell tensor cores. For each MMA weight tile, FlipQuant chooses between two 4-bit element formats, E2M1 (standard FP4) and E0M3 (more mantissa, no exponent). Both quantized candidates are fixed; a binary per-tile format map is learned from end-to-end gradients of a bucketed KL loss against the BF16 teacher, with candidates re-evaluated after each update. Native kernels use 16x64 tiles on SM120 and 256x64 tiles on SM100.

**Result:** Across six language models and two corpora, FlipQuant at 16x64 tiles closes on average 42.5% of NVFP4's perplexity gap to BF16, versus 13.3% for the FourOverSix baseline. Median GEMM overhead is 1.9% (SM120) and 1.7% (SM100).

**Critique:** Results are perplexity only in the abstract; no downstream task accuracy or end-to-end serving throughput. Only FourOverSix is named as a baseline (no comparison to rotation-based methods or other NVFP4 calibration schemes). The six models are not named in the listing abstract, and the calibration cost of learning the format map is not given. Details rest on the abstract as shown in the arXiv cs.LG/new listing (17 Nov); the abstract page itself was not read.

### 5. [Bilateral Log-Linear Attention](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.18974)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.18974
_Kazi Ashik Islam, Guojing Cong_

**In one line:** Bilateral log-linear attention adds an origin-anchored hierarchy; MQAR recall 76.37% to 96.12%, RULER NIAH at 6K 26.97% to 50.03%

**What:** Log-linear attention keeps O(log T) state matrices but its Fenwick-style hierarchy gives fine resolution only near the current query, so early context is coarsely summarized. This paper adds a second hierarchy anchored at the sequence origin, so the two branches together give fine-grained access to both the start and the recent part of the context while staying causal. Recurrent and chunkwise-parallel forms keep log-linear training/prefill work, logarithmic per-token decode work and logarithmic persistent state.

**Result:** Multi-query associative recall: mean test accuracy rises from 76.37% (forward log-linear attention) to 96.12% across seeds. Pretrained LMs at approximately matched parameters: mean on six RULER needle-in-a-haystack tasks at 6144 tokens rises from 26.97% to 50.03%; it beats forward log-linear attention on seven of eight downstream retrieval tasks.

**Critique:** The only baseline named is forward log-linear attention; no comparison to full attention, Mamba-2/Gated DeltaNet or hybrid models in the abstract. 50% on RULER NIAH at only 6K tokens is still low in absolute terms, and model size, token budget and wall-clock kernel speed are not given. 'Approximate parameter matching' leaves room for confounds. Details rest on the abstract as shown in the arXiv cs.LG/new listing (17 Nov); the abstract page itself was not read.

### 6. [Latent Feedback Is a Compute and Data Amplifier](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.20404)

arXiv · paper · scaling · arXiv:2611.20404
_Benhao Huang, Chaitanya Dwivedi, Xuezhe Ma, Alexander Moreno, Hector Liu_

**In one line:** Feeding each token's top-layer state into the next input saves up to 48% compute / 47% unique data vs a tuned Transformer, per scaling fits

**What:** Studies 'latent feedback': each token's final-layer hidden state is fed back into the next token's input embedding, giving the model a recurrent path across positions. It is compared against a vanilla Transformer that spends the same compute on more epochs over the data, with both tuned at every model size and unique-data amount, and a single scaling law is fitted in which latent feedback behaves like a Transformer with more parameters.

**Result:** With unique data fixed, latent feedback reaches the Transformer's loss with less compute; with epochs fixed, it needs less unique data; savings grow with data per parameter and repetition, peaking at 48% of compute and 47% of unique data. The law, fit on 60M-271M models, predicts losses of a held-out 778M model within 0.3% and the gap between the two models within 0.05%.

**Critique:** Scale is small (≤778M). Feeding back the top-layer state makes training sequential across tokens unless approximated, so the 'compute' accounting may not reflect wall-clock or hardware utilization; the listing abstract does not say how training is parallelized. Downstream tasks are said to agree but are not named. Details rest on the abstract as shown in the arXiv cs.LG/new listing (17 Nov); the abstract page itself was not read.

### 7. [MiSTiQ: MicroScaling Trellis Quantization for Extreme KV Cache Compression](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.21216)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.21216
_Ofir Gordon, Lior Dikstein, Paul Balanca, Idan Achituve, Douglas Orr, Hai Victor Habi_

**In one line:** MiSTiQ trellis-codes the KV cache into MX formats; at ~1.25 bits/element beats evaluated VQ baselines on WikiText PPL and GSM8K

**What:** A drop-in replacement for codebook-based KV-cache vector quantizers. It uses trellis-coded storage with beam search over a large reconstruction space instead of large codebooks, and 'MX-Hash' functions that generate reconstruction values directly in a microscaling (MX) element format so dequantized KV can feed low-precision attention kernels.

**Result:** On Llama3 and Qwen3 at about 1.25 effective bits per KV element, it gets lower WikiText perplexity and higher GSM8K accuracy than the evaluated baselines; combined with NVFP4 it reports the best LongBench average among compared methods.

**Critique:** The listing abstract gives no absolute numbers or names for the baselines, and no latency/throughput figures: trellis decoding with beam search can be expensive at encode time, and decode-side kernel cost is the deciding factor for KV compression. Model sizes are not stated. Details rest on the abstract as shown in the arXiv cs.LG/new listing (17 Nov); the abstract page itself was not read.

### 8. [GaugeRoPE: RoPE-Compatible Executable Latent Attention](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.20524)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.20524
_Yuanhang Gao, Xiangrui Yang, Lingyuan Meng, Yuanfeng Chen, Dongsheng Li_

**In one line:** GaugeRoPE runs attention directly on a compressed latent KV cache for RoPE models without retraining: 5.85x KV compression on Mistral-7B

**What:** Latent KV-cache compression for pretrained RoPE models usually has to reconstruct full keys/values per token before applying rotary embeddings, which eats the bandwidth savings. GaugeRoPE specifies when a latent representation can serve directly as the execution state, and gives a training-free construction with a RoPE-compatible carrier plus an approximate residual key-value correction, so attention runs on the compressed state without per-token reconstruction of historical K/V.

**Result:** On Mistral-7B: about 5.85x KV compression with quality close to the full-cache baseline, and up to 2.48x decode throughput over the uncompressed baseline.

**Critique:** One model (Mistral-7B, a 2023 architecture already using GQA) in the abstract; 'close to the full-cache baseline' is not quantified in the listing, and no comparison to other training-free latent methods (e.g. Palu, MLA conversion such as TransMLA) is named. Throughput 'up to' 2.48x likely depends strongly on context length and batch. Details rest on the abstract as shown in the arXiv cs.LG/new listing (17 Nov); the abstract page itself was not read.

### 9. [CertiSum: Bitwise-Identical MXFP4 Training Across Platforms and Parallelism Layouts](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.21584)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.21584
_Steve Rhyner, Yubo Gao, Stefan de Lasa, Sankeerth Durvasula, Nandita Vijaykumar_

**In one line:** CertiSum makes MXFP4 training bitwise identical across MI350X/GB200 and TP/DP/CP layouts at 11.8-60.5% step overhead vs non-exact MXFP4

**What:** Low-precision training results change with GPU vendor and parallelism layout because accumulation order differs. CertiSum certifies most native FP32-accumulated MXFP4 matmul outputs as order-independent using statistics gathered during quantization, recomputes the uncertified outputs exactly, and falls back to exact kernels for shapes where certification rarely succeeds.

**Result:** In 200-step Qwen3-8B-Base runs, training states stayed bitwise identical across AMD MI350X and NVIDIA GB200 and across data, tensor and context parallelism changes on MI350X. For three 8B-14B models on FineWeb-Edu (MI350X), step latency rose 11.8-60.5% versus non-exact MXFP4 at the larger shapes, while still reaching up to 1.24x over BF16 and 1.70x over deterministic BF16.

**Critique:** Only 200-step runs, so long-horizon behavior is untested. The overhead range is wide (up to 60.5%), and the speedups over BF16 are 'up to' figures; which shapes or models fall at each end is not in the listing abstract. Timing results are on MI350X only. Details rest on the abstract as shown in the arXiv cs.LG/new listing (17 Nov); the abstract page itself was not read.

### 10. [CECAR: Cache & Expert Co-Aware Routing Accelerates On-Device Inference of MoE LLMs](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.20119)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.20119
_Byeongju Kim, Hoonki Lee, Byungjun Kim, Inyul Ra, Yongjin Kim, Yongseong Lee et al._

**In one line:** CECAR co-designs MoE routing and expert caching via predicted reuse distance: Qwen3-30B-A3B at 12.5 tok/s on a 16 GB GPU, 4.5x faster

**What:** On-device MoE inference with experts offloaded to SSD is limited by expert cache misses; the authors show hit rates are bounded even under Belady's optimal policy and that prior cache-aware routing using binary cache presence destabilizes routing. CECAR uses a learned cache policy predicting each expert's expected reuse distance, and biases routing and execution toward experts with smaller predicted reuse distance. Accepted to NeurIPS 2026 per the listing.

**Result:** On Qwen3-30B-A3B with 16 GB VRAM on a consumer GPU and non-resident experts fetched from SSD, CECAR reaches 12.5 tokens/s, 4.5x faster than conventional MoE inference, and also exceeds the decode speed of baselines that keep the whole model resident in DRAM.

**Critique:** Changing routing changes the model's outputs; the listing abstract does not quantify accuracy impact. Baselines are described only as categories ('conventional MoE inference', DRAM-resident) rather than named systems such as llama.cpp offload, MoE-Infinity or Fiddler. Single model in the abstract. ### 11. [LoopFill: Pipelined Multi-Token Speculative Decoding for Looped Transformers](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.19649)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.19649
_Siqi Zhu, Suozhi Huang, Yi Zhao, Jiaxuan You_

**In one line:** LoopFill: training-free speculative decoding for looped LMs; 5.85x over greedy on Ouro-2.6B and 5.13x on Huginn-3.5B

**What:** Looped (depth-recurrent) LMs run a shared block many times per token, which makes decoding slow. LoopFill combines multi-token suffix drafts (from context) with predictions read out from shallow loop iterations into a single draft tree, and pipelines verification across loop depth; it requires no training.

**Result:** Average speedup over greedy decoding is 5.85x on Ouro-2.6B and 5.13x on Huginn-3.5B, beating the LoopAhead baseline by 40% and 25% respectively.

**Critique:** Applies only to looped architectures, of which only two small open models are tested. Whether the method is lossless (exact match to greedy) is not stated in the listing summary. Suffix/context drafting gains depend heavily on how repetitive the benchmark prompts are; benchmarks are not named. Author list is as shown in the listing. Details rest on the abstract as shown in the arXiv cs.LG/new listing (17 Nov); the abstract page itself was not read.

**What:** RunAnywhere released TrnRT, an alpha (0.1.0, uploaded 8 Nov; on the HN front page 17 Nov) OpenAI-compatible inference engine for AWS Trainium2. **Result:** Self-reported SemiAnalysis AgentX results on one box at 1M context: 3.57M tokens/min at the default decode batch of 12 (22.3 tok/s per user p50, $0.040 per M tokens at spot), and 46.6 tok/s per user at batch 4. Megakernels cut the batch-8 decode step from 32.2 ms to 24 ms.

**Critique:** All numbers are self-reported and, per the page, come from an earlier build that the maintainers say matches this repository. No comparison against vLLM-Neuron, NxD Inference or GPU serving on the same model. Single supported model and single instance type; alpha status. Quality/accuracy of the FP8 and sparse-attention path versus a reference implementation is not reported.
