### 1. [The Temporal Residual: Fast and Effective Retrofitting of Latent Feedback into Pretrained Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29843)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.29843
_Ziyang Cai, Xingyu Zhu, Sanjeev Arora_

**In one line:** Temporal Residual adds a token-to-token second residual stream (2,752 params) to Qwen3.5-4B-Base; +7/+13/+5 pts on AIME24/AIME25/AMC23

**What:** Transformers cannot pass latent state forward across tokens except through the KV cache and emitted tokens. The paper adds a second residual stream that is carried from one token position to the next, giving latent feedback with only 2,752 extra parameters on a 4.2B model, and retrofits it into a pretrained model with 5B training tokens instead of training from scratch.

**Result:** Retrofitted on Qwen3.5-4B-Base with 5B tokens, it beats a standard model trained on the same data by 7, 13 and 5 points on AIME 2024, AIME 2025 and AMC 2023. Prior designs that feed state back into the existing residual stream recover at most 40% of the gain. GSM8K accuracy with no visible reasoning rises from 21% to 40%.

**Critique:** Single base model at 4B; no evidence at larger scale. AIME sets are tiny (30 problems each), so 5-13 point swings need seeds and confidence intervals, which the abstract does not give. A token-to-token recurrent stream breaks parallel training across positions unless handled carefully; training throughput and inference latency costs are not reported in the abstract. Results read from the listing abstract only.

### 2. [Position-Skipped Distillation of Sparse-Attention Indexers: Train Short, Serve Long](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29664)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.29664
_Fu Dai, Haodi Quan, Kamal Taha_

**In one line:** Distilling top-k sparse-attention indexers on 8K sequences with random position gaps matches 32K/64K-distilled indexers on perplexity

**What:** Learned top-k sparse-attention indexers (as in DeepSeek-style sparse attention) are normally distilled at the target context length, which is expensive. The method distills the indexer on short sequences but inserts randomly placed position gaps, so the indexer sees long-range position offsets without long sequences.

**Result:** On DeepSeek-V2-Lite and Qwen3-30B-A3B, an indexer distilled on 8K tokens is within about 0.5 perplexity of a 32K-distilled indexer up to 32K. On DeepSeek-V2-Lite it matches a 64K-distilled indexer at 64K with roughly 6x less training compute per token. On Llama-3.1-8B it trails the 32K run by 0.6 perplexity. With 4-bit key quantization, perplexity moves by at most 0.05 and the scan is 1.6-2.2x faster than half precision.

**Critique:** Evaluation is perplexity only in the abstract; no retrieval-heavy long-context tasks (needle, RULER, LongBench), where indexer misses matter most. A 0.5-0.6 perplexity gap is not negligible at long context. Contexts stop at 64K. The Llama-3.1-8B result shows the trick is weaker on a dense model.

### 3. [Grammar-as-a-Draft: More Tokens per Speculative Decoding Step on Grammar-Constrained Structured Outputs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28349)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.28349
_John Zhang, Silky Arora_

**In one line:** Grammar-as-a-Draft merges grammar-forced tokens into speculative drafts, raising MTP acceptance length from 2.06 to 5.43 on Qwen3.5-0.8B

**What:** In grammar-constrained decoding (JSON, schemas) many tokens are forced by the grammar, yet speculative drafters still spend proposals and verification on them. GaaD merges the drafter's proposal with grammar-forced tokens and skips logit computation for forced tokens during verification. It works with MTP, DFlash and n-gram drafters.

**Result:** Average acceptance length: 2.06 to 5.43 (MTP, Qwen3.5-0.8B), 1.99 to 5.31 (MTP, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B), 1.57 to 4.13 (DFlash, Qwen3-4B), 1.30 to 3.64 (n-gram, Qwen3-4B). Decoding is 2.66-2.81x faster at batch 1 and 1.14-1.70x at batch 64. A Rust implementation with MTP+GaaD is 2.23x faster on Qwen3.5-0.8B, 2.02x on Qwen3.5-397B-A17B-FP8 and 1.22x at 128 concurrent requests.

**Critique:** The abstract itself notes that committing forced tokens can change the output distribution relative to grammar-masked target decoding, so this is not lossless speculative decoding; the size of that shift is not quantified in the abstract. Gains shrink sharply under batching (1.22x at 128 concurrent). Speedups depend on how much of the output is grammar-forced, which varies by schema.

### 4. [NoHairCache: Calibrate-Once KV Cache Eviction with One Power-Law Exponent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29006)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.29006
_Shuyang Xiang_

**In one line:** NoHairCache treats H2O and StreamingLLM as endpoints of one power-law score; keeps 98.2% of full-cache NarrativeQA accuracy at 25% retention

**What:** KV eviction heuristics differ mainly in how they weight accumulated attention versus recency. The paper scores tokens with a single power-law family whose exponent alpha interpolates between H2O (attention-based) and StreamingLLM (recency-based), and calibrates alpha once on WikiText rather than per task.

**Result:** For Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, calibrated alpha lies in [0.556, 0.822]. It gives the lowest perplexity on WikiText, PG19 and arXiv versus H2O and StreamingLLM, 0.06-0.10 PPL better than StreamingLLM at a 512-token budget. On NarrativeQA it keeps 98.2% of full-cache accuracy at 25% retention versus 91.1% for StreamingLLM.

**Critique:** Single author and a single 1.5B model in the abstract. Baselines are H2O and StreamingLLM only; newer eviction methods (SnapKV, PyramidKV, and the global-importance method covered 23 Nov) are not compared. Perplexity gains of 0.06-0.10 are small. No latency or kernel-compatibility numbers.

### 5. [Muted Distillation: Sealing Reasoning from Teacher Unlocks Token-Efficient Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28445)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · training · arXiv:2611.28445
_Yuwei Zhang, Ian Miao, Wentao Zhang_

**In one line:** Muted Distillation hides student reasoning from the teacher in on-policy distillation, cutting agent output tokens 57% on OfficeQA Pro

**What:** In on-policy distillation (OPD) the teacher scores the student's full trajectory, including its reasoning, which rewards verbose reasoning. Muted Distillation seals the student's reasoning from the teacher, so supervision targets only the visible actions and answers, pushing the student toward shorter episodes.

**Result:** Versus standard OPD: output tokens per episode down 57% on OfficeQA Pro and 53% on SpreadsheetBench; accuracy matches OPD on OfficeQA Pro and pass@1 improves 4.8 points on SpreadsheetBench.

**Critique:** Only two agent benchmarks, both office/spreadsheet tasks. The only baseline in the abstract is standard OPD; length-penalized RL or simple length-controlled OPD would be the natural comparison. Model sizes and wall-clock savings are not given in the abstract.

### 6. [RES: Accelerating Diffusion Language Models with Rolling Entropy Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29066)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.29066
_Patrick Pynadath, Nicolas Perez-Nieves, Bobak Shahriari_

**In one line:** Training-free Rolling Entropy Sampling raises diffusion-LM tokens per forward pass from 19.3 to 25.5, with macro accuracy 80.7% to 78.8%

**What:** Diffusion LMs decode a window of masked tokens in parallel; samplers decide which positions to commit each step. RES commits a contiguous accepted prefix of the denoising window once it reaches a minimum length, while keeping positions further ahead revisable, so the window rolls forward. No training is needed.

**Result:** Across seven benchmarks, versus the entropy-bound sampler in DiffusionGemma: tokens per forward pass 19.3 to 25.5 (+32%), macro accuracy 80.7% to 78.8%.

**Critique:** It trades about 1.9 points of accuracy for speed, so it is a new point on the speed/quality curve, not a free win; a fair comparison would match accuracy and compare speed. Tokens per forward pass is not wall-clock throughput. Single baseline sampler and apparently a single model family.

Hugging Face model card · release · efficiency · arXiv:2611.26157

### 8. [Estimating Silenced Tokens in Quantized LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29536)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.29536
_Giuseppe Pappalardo_

**In one line:** Certified bounds show quantized LM heads can make hundreds of tokens unreachable as top-1, already at 8-bit on a 1B model

**What:** Quantizing the output head can make some vocabulary tokens impossible to emit as the argmax, whatever the input. The paper computes certified lower and upper bounds on the number of such 'silenced' tokens for a given quantized head.

**Result:** Silencing appears already at 8-bit on large-vocabulary models, up to a few hundred previously reachable tokens on a 1B-parameter model, and grows as precision drops. A small-vocabulary model resists until 3 bits. On a multilingual vocabulary, the silenced tokens are mostly non-Latin.

**Critique:** Single author; no named baselines and model names not given in the abstract. Top-1 unreachability matters for greedy decoding but less under sampling, and the abstract does not measure downstream impact. Practical deployments often keep the LM head at higher precision, which would sidestep the issue; worth checking whether the setups studied do so.

### 9. [KernelShift: Benchmarking Kernel Optimization Across Evolving Hardware](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29590)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.29590
_Shuaiwei Huang, Weihua Du, Allen Wu_

**In one line:** KernelShift: agent-written Arm CPU kernels beat auto-vectorization 2.4-19.8x but reach only 0.70x of hand-tuned KleidiAI

**What:** A benchmark of 99 CPU kernels drawn from ncnn, llama.cpp, Arm SIMD Loops and KleidiAI, spanning Arm Neon, SVE, SVE2 and SME2, to test whether LLM agents can write kernels for new instruction sets.

**Result:** Agents beat the auto-vectorizing compiler by 2.4-19.8x and reach up to 2.74x over ncnn's Neon kernels, but only 0.70x of Arm's hand-tuned KleidiAI kernels. In end-to-end inference of two LLMs, agent-written kernels give 1.3-1.6x prefill speedup over llama.cpp's fast path.

**Critique:** Which agents/models were evaluated and on what hardware is not in the abstract. Beating compiler auto-vectorization is a weak bar; the 0.70x versus KleidiAI is the more telling number. Prefill speedups over llama.cpp may reflect llama.cpp not using the newest instructions (e.g. SME2) rather than agent skill. Correctness checking across inputs is not described in the abstract.

### 10. [TLX: A hardware-native GPU compiler for production ML (Meta Triton Low-Level Extensions)](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/11/10/open-source/tlx-a-hardware-native-gpu-compiler-for-production-ml/)

Engineering at Meta blog · news · efficiency

**In one line:** Meta details TLX, Triton extensions for warp-group orchestration, TMA/TMEM and clusters on Hopper, Blackwell, MI300 and MI350x

**What:** TLX (Triton Low-Level Extensions) keeps Triton's Python model for computation but exposes explicit orchestration: warp groups as first-class producer/consumer/epilogue roles, mbarrier-level synchronization, direct TMA, async tensor-core ops and Blackwell TMEM, and multi-CTA clusters with distributed shared memory. Meta says it powers production training, including the GEM model and a distributed grouped GEMM for MoE layers in its LLM pretraining stack. **Result:** Per Meta's post: 20-25% end-to-end MFU on thousands of GPUs for GEM training, up to 2x forward speedup for its GDPA attention kernel, 4x on H100 for the IKBO linear compression kernel, and up to two-thirds lower compute-intensive net latency for co-designed models.

**Critique:** The post gives no baselines for its speedups, so '2x' and '4x' cannot be judged; it says TLX is 'typically' on par with hand-tuned CUDA without numbers. No license or repository link is stated on the page despite the Open Source category. The post is two weeks old; the cuBLAS/rocBLAS comparisons rest on secondary coverage and are self-reported.

### 11. [Language Model Harnesses can be Compositional Generalizers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28298)

arXiv cs.CL · paper · other · arXiv:2611.28298
_Alex L. Zhang, Souradip Chakraborty, Omar Khattab_

**In one line:** RL-tuned recursive LM harness trained on short tasks generalizes to 2-64x longer ones; RLM-27B +26.9% over Qwen3.8-27B in same harness

**What:** Instead of extending context, a recursive language-model (RLM) harness decomposes long tasks into calls. The paper RL-trains the model inside the harness on short tasks, using surrogate harness training on synthetic data, and tests generalization to much longer tasks.

**Result:** Training on short tasks generalizes to tasks 2-64x longer. RLM-27B improves on Qwen3.8-27B in the same harness by 26.9% on average on CTC-Bench and ExpertLongBench while using about 2x fewer inference tokens.

**Critique:** Off focus but relevant to long-context alternatives. The baseline is the untrained model in the same harness, not a long-context model reading everything or other agentic scaffolds. Two benchmarks; '26.9%' is an average whose relative/absolute nature is unclear from the abstract.
