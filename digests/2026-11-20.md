### 1. [Toaster: Nonlinear Memory from Cascaded Linear Memories and Target Propagation](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.23648)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.23648
_Zeman Li, Ali Behrouz, Yuan Deng, Praneeth Kacham_

**In one line:** Toaster stacks two DeltaNet-style linear memories with target propagation; beats KDA at all five sizes from 0.2B to 1.6B

**What:** Linear-attention/delta-rule models keep a single linear associative memory. Toaster builds a two-layer nonlinear memory out of two DeltaNet-style linear memories and trains it with target propagation, so each layer can still run an unmodified delta-rule kernel rather than needing a custom nonlinear-memory kernel.

**Result:** At matched budgets it has lower loss than KDA at all five sizes (0.2B-1.6B). At the largest size, average 3-shot accuracy over 13 tasks is 51.84% vs 50.52% for KDA and 50.04% for Titans. Loss stays below KDA's up to 20K tokens (5x the training length).

**Critique:** Numbers come from the listing abstract as read via the fetch tool; the abstract page and full paper were not read. Largest model is 1.6B, and the accuracy gap over KDA is about 1.3 points. Throughput/wall-clock cost of running two cascaded memories versus one is not given in the abstract, and no comparison with hybrid (attention + linear) models is mentioned.

### 2. [QDA: Closed-Loop LLM Quantization Design with Agents](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.23388)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.23388
_Xingyang Li, Tianlang Zhao, Song Han, Ligeng Zhu_

**In one line:** QDA uses an agent to design PTQ recipes scored by output KL; recovers 98.3% of FP8 accuracy on a 320B MoE at NVFP4 W4A4

**What:** Post-training quantization recipes (which layers, formats, rotation/clipping choices) are usually hand-tuned. QDA puts an LLM agent in a closed loop that proposes PTQ recipes and scores each by the KL divergence of the quantized model's outputs against the full-precision model, iterating toward better recipes.

**Result:** Beats the strongest public recipe by 0.9, 1.4 and 2.2 points on average at W4A16, W4A8 and W3A16. On the 320B MoE GLM-5.3-Flash at NVFP4 W4A4 it recovers 98.3% of FP8 accuracy vs 96.9% for the best vendor checkpoint.

**Critique:** Numbers come from the listing abstract as read via the fetch tool; the abstract page and full paper were not read. The search cost (agent calls, GPU-hours of calibration/evaluation per recipe) is not stated in the abstract, which matters for a 320B model. Risk of overfitting the recipe to the evaluation suite, and how transferable discovered recipes are across models, are not addressed in the abstract.

### 4. [NarrowSpec: Draft Hidden Width Need Not Match the Target in Speculative Decoding](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.23835)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.23835
_Alagappan Valliappan, Ankit More, Dheevatsa Mudigere, Jatin Mitra_

**In one line:** NarrowSpec halves or quarters draft-model hidden width with acceptance kept; 1.13-1.44x faster decode on GB10 at batch 1

**What:** Speculative-decoding drafters (DFlash, EAGLE-3) usually match the target's hidden width so they can consume its features. NarrowSpec shows the draft hidden width can be cut to 1/2 or 1/4 of the target's while keeping acceptance, reducing drafter cost. **Result:** At batch 1: 1.06-1.12x decode speedup on B200 and 1.13-1.44x on GB10 over full-width drafters. From batch 32 up to the largest batch each GPU fits, speedup reaches 1.21x.

**Critique:** Numbers come from the listing abstract as read via the fetch tool; the abstract page and full paper were not read. Gains on B200 at batch 1 are small (6-12%). Only two mid-size targets were tested; whether narrow drafters hold acceptance on larger or reasoning-heavy targets and longer outputs is not shown in the abstract.

### 5. [Equal Steps, Unequal Curvature: Where Muon's Update Goes Wrong](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.24644)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.24644
_Biswa Sengupta, Jinhua Wang, Léo Maxime Brunswic_

**In one line:** Curvature analysis of Muon: orthogonalizing Q, K, V separately cuts loss 0.0250 nats on a 124M model; spectral tilt adds more

**What:** Muon orthogonalizes each weight-matrix update, giving equal step sizes across singular directions even when curvature differs. The paper analyses where this mismatch hurts and proposes fixes: orthogonalizing Q, K and V separately instead of a fused QKV matrix, a spectral tilt (sigma^-1/4) during cooldown, and energy-matched Schatten-1 weight decay.

**Result:** On a 124M decoder trained on FineWeb-Edu: separate Q/K/V orthogonalization lowers loss by 0.0250 nats; spectral tilt in cooldown gives a further 0.0120 nats, and 0.0158 nats combined with NorMuon (three seeds); the tilt gives 0.0188 nats at 355M. Schatten-1 weight decay adds 0.0052 nats.

**Critique:** Numbers come from the listing abstract as read via the fetch tool; the abstract page and full paper were not read. Scale is 124M-355M, far below where optimizer choices for frontier pretraining are decided; gains are in nats of loss with no downstream evaluations mentioned. Only three seeds for the NorMuon combination; seed variance for other numbers is not stated.

### 7. [LoopCache: Accelerating Long-Context Self-Speculative Decoding for Looped Transformers](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.24301)

**In one line:** LoopCache adds a cyclic loop-KV cache to self-speculative decoding in looped Transformers; 10.25x average speedup over WFD

**What:** Looped (depth-recurrent) Transformers reuse the same block several times, so self-speculative decoding at long context must read KV for every loop iteration. LoopCache keeps a cyclic loop-KV cache that limits per-step KV traffic to a single loop's worth.
