### 1. [LoCoJAX: Host-Local Coordination for Small-Model RL on TPUs](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.16301)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.16301
_Loki Chen, Gregory B. Rehm, Yang Song, Hongye Xie, Ming Lei, Chongyang Bai et al._

**In one line:** LoCoJAX runs RL rollouts and rewards locally on each TPU host: 1.3-3.6x faster steps than tuned Pathways for Qwen3-0.6B

**What:** Profiles RL post-training of dense models from 0.6B to 405B on TPU v7x with a decode-heavy workload and finds that for small models the single Pathways controller's host orchestration eats 36% of step time (network only 2%), while for large models network cost dominates. LoCoJAX moves rollout generation and reward processing onto each TPU host while keeping the training step globally synchronous. Two modes: colocated (LoCoJAX-CO, rollout and training share hosts) and disaggregated (LoCoJAX-DA, Ray-managed separate rollout and training hosts that overlap).

**Result:** Against a tuned Pathways baseline on Qwen3-0.6B, across three workloads and 8-64 TPU v7x chips, LoCoJAX-CO is 1.3-3.6x faster per step; on GSM8K, 8 chips with LoCoJAX-CO finish a step faster than Pathways on 64 chips. LoCoJAX-DA helps only when rollout dominates: on NuminaMath-CoT at 256 chips, giving rollout 75% of hosts cuts step time by about 15% vs LoCoJAX-CO.

**Critique:** Read from the cs.LG listing abstract only; individual abstract pages were not fetched this run. The headline speedups are for a 0.6B model, where controller overhead is by construction largest; the abstract gives no LoCoJAX numbers for larger models and does not state the model size of the 256-chip run. Comparison is only against Pathways-based RL, not against GPU-style frameworks (e.g. verl/OpenRLHF-like designs) that already colocate rollout. TPU-specific, and no learning-curve or final-accuracy comparison is described.

### 2. [metalSPARSE: A 2:4 Semi-Structured Sparse GEMV Kernel for Apple Silicon GPUs](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.14545)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.14545
_Tek Raj Awasthi, Babita Awasthi_

**In one line:** metalSPARSE 2:4 sparse Metal kernels decode Qwen2.5-1.5B 1.52x faster than MLX on an M4; perplexity gap cut to 1.34x dense

**What:** Apple GPUs have no sparse tensor hardware, so 2:4 pruning only helps memory-bound decoding if the kernel reads fewer bytes. metalSPARSE stores 2:4-pruned weights compressed, decompresses them in registers inside a fused Metal GEMV decoder, and applies 2:4 sparsity to every decoder linear layer. Quality recovery combines a Wanda mask with block-wise reconstruction and jointly trained low-rank adapters quantized to 4 bits, all run on the laptop itself.

**Result:** Qwen2.5-1.5B decoding is 3.6x faster than HuggingFace Transformers on PyTorch-MPS (8 GB M2 MacBook Air) and 1.52x faster than MLX (16 GB M4); the same engine with dense weights matches MLX, and the kernel reaches 97% of the speedup the byte reduction allows. WikiText-2 perplexity goes from 3.01x dense (one-shot Wanda with closed-form refit) to 1.34x dense. Prefill sees no speedup.

**Critique:** Read from the cs.LG listing abstract only; individual abstract pages were not fetched this run. A 1.34x perplexity ratio is still a large quality loss, and no downstream benchmarks are reported. Single small model (1.5B); no comparison with dense 4-bit quantization at equal bytes, which is the obvious alternative for bandwidth-bound decode on Macs. The 3.6x figure is against an unoptimized PyTorch-MPS baseline; the MLX comparison (1.52x) is the meaningful one.

### 3. [Probabilistic State Space Enumeration in Neural Network Computation](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.15062)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.15062
_Victor O. K. Li, Yang Han, Jacqueline C. K. Lam_

**In one line:** Writing a Transformer MLP as an expectation over channel-group states: 92.4% output accuracy at 2048 states vs 76.9% for Top-k

**What:** Rewrites a Transformer MLP exactly as an expectation over binary inclusion states of channel groups: the intermediate dimension is split into groups, an input-dependent distribution over which groups are active is defined, and importance-corrected masked outputs make the expectation equal the dense output. NORDER-II enumerates joint states in decreasing probability without building or sorting the exponential space, so truncating the enumeration (by cumulative probability mass or a state budget) gives an anytime approximation of the MLP, i.e. a principled form of activation sparsity.

**Result:** At a budget of 2048 states, MLP-output approximation accuracy is 92.4% (Llama-3.2-1B) and 93.8% (Gemma-2-2B) vs 76.9% and 79.8% for Channel Top-k. Approximating the first, middle and last MLP layers jointly keeps WikiText-2 perplexity within 0.06% and 0.54% of dense. Relative accuracy gains of 82.6-126.4% over expected-count Grouped Top-k at 0.18-0.19% extra analytic FLOPs.

**Critique:** Read from the cs.LG listing abstract only; individual abstract pages were not fetched this run. No wall-clock latency or throughput is reported, and evaluating up to 2048 masked states per token may cost far more than the dense MLP unless they share computation; the efficiency case is therefore unproven. Only three of the MLP layers are approximated in the perplexity test, on 1-2B models, with WikiText-2 as the only quality metric. Baselines are simple Top-k variants, not trained activation-sparsity methods (e.g. predictor-based or CATS/TEAL-style).

### 4. [Recompute or Restore? Host-Tier KV for Multi-Model Agent Workflows on a Static-Graph NPU Serving Stack](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.15126)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.15126
_Sungwon Woo, Seyeon Kim_

**In one line:** Host-DRAM KV tier on a Rebellions NPU lifts agent-workflow SLO attainment from 22.8% to 100% under overload; 89.5% of prompts reused

**What:** Studies when restoring a reused KV prefix from host memory beats recomputing it on a commercial static-graph NPU stack (Rebellions ATOM+), for an agent workflow pairing a Qwen3-8B planner with a Qwen3-0.6B executor. Finds 89.5% of planner prompt tokens had already been computed, so repeated prefill dominates when serving is overloaded. Adds a synchronous host-DRAM KV tier plus a scheduler-aware latency model that decides restore vs recompute.

**Result:** Workflow SLO attainment rises from 22.8% to 100% and from 9.4% to 99.8% in two overload conditions. On fresh seeds, conservative gains are 11.8% (chain) and 18.2% (fork) workflows; mixed and bursty workloads vary by seed. The latency model predicts median and P99 within 15% across 24 conditions but misorders three policy comparisons and predicts 11% benefit for a cheaper store path where 3.1% was measured.

**Critique:** Read from the cs.LG listing abstract only; individual abstract pages were not fetched this run. Single vendor stack and one model pair; the dramatic SLO numbers come from overload regimes chosen to stress recompute, while realistic-load gains are 12-18% and seed-dependent. The authors themselves report the latency model misranking policies. No comparison against existing GPU KV-offload systems (e.g. LMCache-style tiers), so how much is NPU-specific is unclear.

### 5. [Mask-Pattern Position Augmentation: Training-Free Long-Context Extrapolation for Diffusion LLMs](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.15029)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.15029
_Gaurav Sarkar, Syed Affan Daimi_

**In one line:** Training-free MPPA extends LLaDA-8B to 2x its window: perplexity at 8192 tokens 3.12 vs 19.51, 100% passkey retrieval

**What:** Diffusion LLMs inherit RoPE's fixed window, and the prior fix (LongLLaDA) borrows static NTK-style RoPE scaling from autoregressive models, ignoring that a diffusion LM's input is partially masked and changes every denoising step. MPPA linearly interpolates position indices into the pretrained window, then adds a per-position, per-step offset derived from cumulative mask density to restore the rotary phase resolution that interpolation collapses; the offset shrinks as tokens are committed, so late steps see near-clean interpolated positions.

**Result:** On LLaDA-8B-Instruct at 8192 tokens (2x its 4096 window), perplexity drops from 19.51 under standard decoding to 3.12, reported as below the in-window value; passkey retrieval reaches 100%; needle-in-a-haystack accuracy is unchanged.

**Critique:** Read from the cs.LG listing abstract only; individual abstract pages were not fetched this run. The authors call these preliminary 2x results; no tests beyond 8K or on other diffusion LMs. The headline comparison is against unmodified decoding, and the abstract gives no numbers for the LongLLaDA baseline it argues against. Perplexity plus passkey are weak long-context evidence; no RULER/LongBench-style tasks.

### 6. [Is the Importance Ratio Necessary for Stable Reinforcement Learning in LLMs?](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.15404)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.15404
_Shuibai Zhang, Junhyuck Kim, Gyeongman Kim, Jaewoong Cho_

**In one line:** LGPO drops GRPO's old-policy likelihood pass and gates updates by likelihood: ~18% less training time, stays stable fully on-policy

**What:** GRPO-style RL computes likelihoods under both old and current policies to form importance ratios. The paper separates the two roles of the ratio: importance correction and trust region. Likelihood-Gated Policy Optimization (LGPO) enforces a soft trust region by gating on the current likelihood instead, so the old-policy forward pass is no longer needed. It also notes that in fully on-policy training the ratio is identically 1, so ratio clipping never activates.

**Result:** Where GRPO is stable, LGPO matches its stability and peak performance while cutting training time by about 18% on average vs a standard implementation that recomputes old-policy likelihoods. In fully on-policy training, where GRPO collapses, LGPO stays stable. Removing the importance correction alone did not hurt stability; removing the trust region did.

**Critique:** Read from the cs.LG listing abstract only; individual abstract pages were not fetched this run. The abstract names no models, sizes or benchmarks, so the scope of 'comparable peak performance' is unknown. The 18% saving is relative to an implementation that recomputes old-policy likelihoods; implementations that cache them during rollout already avoid part of that cost. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 per the listing comments.

### 8. [Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.17334)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.17334
_Ellis Brown, Chris Hoang, Shengyi Qian, Shang-Wen Li, Mike Lewis, Marjan Ghazvininejad et al._

**In one line:** Matched-compute study: a pretrained vision encoder isn't needed, but dedicated vision capacity placed early in the backbone is

**What:** Pretrained vision encoders in multimodal LLMs bundle three choices: upstream visual pretraining, dedicated vision parameters, and placing them before multimodal fusion. The authors disentangle these by pretraining all variants from scratch on common data at matched compute, comparing encoder-based, encoder-free, and in-backbone early vision-module designs.

**Result:** Qualitative findings only in the abstract: the visual prior from upstream pretraining is not necessary; an encoder-free model trails on visual understanding even when its backbone is enlarged by the encoder's parameter count; vision modules placed early inside the backbone perform comparably to a pre-fusion encoder and can lower time to first token. No numbers are given.

**Critique:** Read from the cs.LG listing abstract only; individual abstract pages were not fetched this run. No benchmark scores, model sizes or compute budgets in the abstract, so effect sizes cannot be judged. Matched-compute from-scratch training is a clean design but may favour no-prior variants at budgets where pretrained encoders' advantage (trained on far more images) is hidden by the shared data. TTFT benefit is stated as possible, not measured in the abstract. Affiliations not verified beyond the author list (Lewis, Zettlemoyer, Beyer, Xie et al.).

**What:** Walkthrough (13 Nov) of serving the 464 GB GLM-5.3 NVFP4 MoE checkpoint, too large for one GB300's 252 GB HBM3e, by tensor parallelism across two DGX Stations linked by 400 Gb/s ConnectX-8 RoCE with GPU Direct RDMA (about 218 GiB of weights per GPU). ### 11. [Faster Scientific Simulations with New FP64 Algorithms in NVIDIA cuBLAS](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/faster-scientific-simulations-with-new-fp64-algorithms-in-nvidia-cublas/)

NVIDIA Technical Blog · release · other

**In one line:** cuBLAS adds Ozaki-II FP64 emulation on integer Tensor Cores: DGEMM 3.9x native on GB200, 5.5x on Vera Rubin NVL72

**What:** CUDA Toolkit 13.4.2 cuBLAS adds Ozaki-II, which emulates double-precision GEMM on integer Tensor Cores using the Chinese Remainder Theorem to need fewer integer multiplications than Ozaki-I, and a 2M method that computes complex GEMM with two real GEMMs instead of four. Both sit in cuBLAS's Automatic Dynamic Precision framework, which falls back to native FP64 if the required mantissa bits exceed the allowed budget. Relevant to kernel work as an example of trading low-precision tensor-core throughput for high-precision results.

**Result:** DGEMM: 5.5x over native FP64 on VR NVL72, 3.9x on GB200 (2x Ozaki-I), up to 23x on RTX PRO 6000; peak 175/147/37 TFLOPS. ZGEMM 2M: 5x on GB200. End-to-end CCSD 2x (VR NVL72) and 1.4x (GB200) in full FP64; BerkeleyGW 6.6x and 3.9x.

**Critique:** Vendor-reported numbers on NVIDIA's own selected workloads; accuracy is claimed comparable to native FP64 via the ADP fallback, but ill-conditioned inputs where emulation needs many more slices are not characterized. The large RTX PRO 6000 figure mostly reflects that card's weak native FP64. Off focus for LLM work: aimed at scientific computing.
