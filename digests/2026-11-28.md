### 1. [Mixture-of-Loops Transformer](https://github.com/hustvl/MoLT)

GitHub (hustvl) / arXiv cs.LG · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.34923
_Lianghui Zhu, Shijie Wang, Xinggang Wang, Jinze Bai, Bo Wang, Xiang Bai_

**In one line:** MoLT: looped Transformer passes KV memory across loops; U-MoLT claims 1.7x token efficiency vs OLMo3 at 700M on 400B tokens

**What:** Looped Transformers reuse shared layers across loops but pay for it in efficiency; the paper asks what should be passed between loops. It introduces Mixture-of-Loops Attention (MoLA), which treats KV as memory across depths and loops. MoLT-R keeps the residual stream and reads local KV across loops; MoLT-KV restarts each loop from the embeddings and reads current-loop local KV plus preceding-loop memory KV at earlier positions. 'Loop forcing' allows parallel training and single-traversal Mixture-of-Loops decoding. U-MoLT splits memory construction (a downward encoder) from prediction readout (an upward cross-attention-only decoder) and can skip unused decoder traversals at inference without changing predictions.

**Result:** Per the README: with 400B-token pretraining against OLMo3 baselines, U-MoLT reaches 1.7x validation cross-entropy token efficiency at 700M; downstream average 62.6 vs 61.2 for OLMo3-1.5B, and 62.2 vs 60.1 for a 3B MoE (660M active), where 10-domain perplexity improves by 0.75 and 10-task accuracy by 2.1 points. Continued pretraining of Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base for 100B tokens gives 1.4x mean token efficiency. At ~113M parameters, U-MoLT with MoL-Hybrid-2 decoding runs at 996.6 ms vs 1,000.6 ms for non-looped OLMo3.

**Critique:** Details come from the GitHub README; the arXiv abstract page and the full paper were not read (the HF paper page fetch was declined). Training compute is not reported. The latency comparison is at ~113M parameters only and shows parity rather than a speedup. Only a minimal S-size U-MoLT implementation and a fused MoLA Triton kernel are released; MoLT-R, MoLT-KV, MoL-Hybrid decoding and the large-scale recipes are still TODO, and no checkpoints are listed, so the 700M-3B results cannot yet be reproduced.

### 2. [LoopTitan: What Does Parameter Revisit Buy a Mixture-of-Experts?](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34821)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.34821
_Zichen Tian, Yudong Lu, Xuansheng Wu_

**In one line:** LoopTitan: in looped MoEs, parameter revisit adds ~nothing (+0.7 mBPB); gains come from shape, residual scaling and the optimizer

**What:** An ablation study of looped mixture-of-experts. Reported gains of looped MoEs over standard MoEs confound parameter revisit with network shape and residual scaling. The authors build non-revisit controls with the same shape, expert layout and residual scaling but independent parameters, plus a deeper control at equal parameters and compute (18 layers of width 1280, 42 experts per layer instead of 64). They show residual scaling is equivalent to an optimizer setting. They release the LoopTitan codebase, which unifies nine published looped recipes and checks parameter, compute and KV-cache budgets, and LoopTitan-Base, a standard MoE that matches the looped recipe under Muon without revisit.

**Result:** At 1.3B stored / 0.2B active parameters and 5B tokens (mBPB, negative is better): the looped MoE beats MoE-12 by -6.3 under AdamW, but the non-revisit control gets -7.0, so revisit alone contributes +0.7 (noise band ±2.1). Shape change accounts for -4.5 and expert layout plus residual scaling for -2.6. Switching MoE-12 to Muon alone gives -23.6, 3.7x the AdamW looped-recipe gain. Under Muon, adding one visit (+31% compute) gains 11.5 vs 10.4 for the deeper control, of which revisit explains 1.1. LoopTitan-Base is within 0.2 mBPB of the looped MoE.

**Critique:** Small scale: main runs are 1.3B/0.2B-active on 5B tokens, with larger checks at 3.0B/0.5B-active on 12-24B tokens and longer runs on a single seed; conclusions may not hold at frontier token counts. Only the first ~40K of ~885K characters were read verbatim, so the main results table was not checked. Results are loss (mBPB) only, with no downstream or inference-latency evaluation in the part read. GPU-hours are not stated, and no repository URL appeared in the read text.

**What:** Final release of the Raon-1 mini model, which the card says was trained from scratch; the af.net release tracker dates it 26 Nov. ### 5. [Any-Rate: Half-Bit Multi-Precision LLM Quantization with Coded Bit-Planes](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34402v1)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.34402
_Hoonki Lee, Haechan Shin, Hyuntaek Jung_

**In one line:** Any-Rate: one PTQ checkpoint serves 2-8-bit LLM weights in half-bit steps over 2-4 bits via Hamming-coded bit-planes

**What:** Nested multi-precision quantization derives several precisions from one checkpoint by reading leading bit-planes, but existing methods offer only integer widths and degrade at 2 bits. Any-Rate is post-training quantization that constrains some bit-planes to extended Hamming [8,4,4] codewords over blocks of 8 weights, so each coded plane costs 0.5 bits/weight. A UHHHHHH layout (one unconstrained plane plus six coded planes) yields 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5 and 4-bit modes, and residual planes extend it to 5-8 bits. One lookup table per row is shared across modes, and all modes are encoded jointly with per-mode error feedback.

**Result:** Abstract: across four LLMs, state of the art among multi-precision methods at 2 and 3 bits. It keeps 69% of FP16 accuracy at 2 bits and is within one point of the best at 4 bits, against Any-Precision LLM, AnyBCQ and MatGPTQ. Decoding speed is about the same as existing kernels, and an LPDDR energy model estimates up to 27% less energy at equal accuracy. From the summarized body (not verbatim): Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct at 2 bits decodes at 142.4 tok/s vs 141.6 for Any-Precision LLM on an RTX 5070 Ti.

**Critique:** Models are small (Qwen2.5-3B, Gemma-3-4b-it, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Phi-4 14B), so the method is untested on large MoEs. 69% of FP16 accuracy at 2 bits is still a large loss. The energy saving is an estimate from memory traffic, not a measurement. It trails MatGPTQ (3/4/8) slightly at 4 bits. Only ~40K of ~466K characters were read verbatim, so the main tables were not checked. Code: github.com/hoonki2002/Any-Rate.

### 6. [Learning to Be Truncated: Preparing LLM Checkpoints for SVD Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34667)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.34667
_Chenyue Fang, Danila Mishin, Babak Falsafi_

**In one line:** LLift prepares one LLM checkpoint so a single SVD can be truncated to several compression ratios without per-ratio fine-tuning

**What:** SVD-based LLM compression usually needs fine-tuning or weight correction, repeated for every compression ratio. LLift fine-tunes a low-rank approximation at one ratio, using the original model as teacher. It then merges the learned updates back into the original weights while keeping the complementary components that were excluded from the training forward pass. SVD is applied once to the prepared model's target matrices, and the decompositions are reused at different truncation ranks.

**Result:** The abstract gives no numbers. It reports accuracy gains across three compression ratios on LLaMA-7B from a single prepared model, and says the gains extend across model families, sizes and SVD operators.

**Critique:** Only the abstract was read, and it states no figures, baselines or compute. The headline experiment uses LLaMA-7B, an old base model. The abstract does not say whether perplexity and accuracy hold up at aggressive ratios or how it compares with SVD-LLM-style methods. No code is linked.

### 8. [Lightweight Reasoning Transfer via Chain-of-Thought Vector Projection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34183)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.34183
_Yulin Qi, Shuo Xu, Shuxia Lin_

**In one line:** CoT-vector projection moves a teacher's reasoning steering vector into a smaller student's residual stream, no distillation run

**What:** A lightweight alternative to distilling reasoning into a smaller model: a teacher-side 'CoT Vector' (a steering direction associated with chain-of-thought behaviour) is projected into the residual-stream space of a smaller student. The listing abstract describes transfer through the student's activations rather than a full distillation run; how and where the projected vector is applied is not detailed in the abstract as read.

**Result:** Per the abstract: +10.37 to +21.98 accuracy points on GSM8K and MATH across teacher-student settings. On four code benchmarks, +3.23 to +4.67 pass@1 for a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct student and up to +9.65 for base Qwen2.5-7B.

**Critique:** From the listing abstract only. Gains are relative to the unsteered student, not to standard logit or on-policy distillation or to simply prompting for CoT, so whether this beats cheap baselines is unclear. Students are ~7B Qwen2.5 models, and the abstract does not say how the projection is learned or how much data it needs. It is borderline on focus: it is reasoning transfer via activation steering, not compression.
