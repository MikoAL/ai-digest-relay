### 1. [StrataFlux: Memory-Hierarchy-Native Low-Bit LLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08363)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.08363
_Jake Hyun, Ziyu Zhao, Jae-sun Seo, Mohamed S. Abdelfattah_

**In one line:** StrataFlux streams extra weight/KV precision from host RAM and SSD: 2-bit Qwen3-8B AIME 11.0%→47.1% at unchanged GPU memory

**What:** Low-bit weight and KV-cache quantization saves GPU memory but loses accuracy. StrataFlux keeps the low-bit tensors resident on the GPU and treats host RAM and SSD as stores of the residual precision, streaming that extra precision back only selectively to cut transfer volume, and overlapping the transfers with computation so the higher-precision correction does not stall decoding.

**Result:** 2-bit Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct weights: WikiText-2 perplexity 9.46→8.40 while keeping 95.4% of the quantized model's throughput on an RTX 5090. Qwen3-8B with 2-bit weights and KV cache: AIME 2024/2025 accuracy 11.0%→47.1% at the same GPU memory. At 128K context, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with 3-bit weights and 2-bit KV matches vLLM with 4-bit AWQ weights and 8-bit KV on perplexity and speed using about half the GPU memory.

**Critique:** Evaluated on 8B models and a single consumer GPU; whether PCIe/SSD bandwidth keeps up at larger batch sizes or on multi-GPU servers is not shown in the abstract. The AIME jump is measured from a very degraded 2-bit baseline, and the abstract does not compare against stronger 2-bit methods (e.g. QTIP- or vector-quantization-based) at equal memory. Host RAM and SSD capacity used is a real cost that 'unchanged GPU memory' hides. No code link on the abstract page.

### 2. [D3D: Decoupled Diffusion Drafting for Asynchronous Speculative Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06425)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.06425
_Baoyou Chen, Ruiqiao Mei, Guancong Lin, Tao Song, Weihao Yuan, Qun Liu et al._

**In one line:** D3D: target-independent diffusion drafter drafts during verification; 5.00x avg speedup on Qwen3-8B vs DFlash's 4.03x

**What:** Target-conditioned block-diffusion drafters (e.g. DFlash) need the target's latest hidden states before drafting the next block, so drafting and verification are serialized. D3D is a standalone diffusion drafter trained with blockwise denoising plus auxiliary autoregressive supervision, so it needs no target hidden states; it can therefore draft successor blocks in one packed pass while the target verifies the current one. Layers are pruned by their effect on acceptance length to make packed drafting cheaper (5 of 36 layers removed keeps >99% of mean acceptance length with a Qwen3-8B target).

**Result:** Across seven benchmarks: 5.00x average decoding speedup with Qwen3-8B (DFlash 4.03x) and 5.71x with Qwen3-32B (DFlash 3.67x); up to 7.48x on GSM8K with Qwen3-32B.

**Critique:** Only one family of targets (Qwen3) and one baseline (DFlash) are named in the abstract; EAGLE-style and other asynchronous/parallel drafting methods are not reported there. Speedups are almost certainly at batch size 1, where idle compute makes packed drafting cheap; behaviour under batched serving is not shown. GSM8K-style tasks are favourable to long acceptance. Drafter training cost is not stated. Code is released (fudan-generative-vision/D3D).

### 3. [TriAttention: Unified Sparse Mixed-Precision Attention for Long-Context LLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07256)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.07256
_Hanzhi Zhang, Qiao Zhang, Qinglei Cao, Heng Fan, Yan Huang, Kewei Sha et al._

**In one line:** TriAttention assigns each attention tile to skip, INT4 or INT8 via two per-head diagonal boundaries; up to 2.65x over BF16

**What:** Combines sparsity and quantization in one attention kernel: each query-key tile is either skipped, computed in 4-bit, or computed in 8-bit. The policy is encoded compactly as two diagonal-distance boundaries per head (so precision depends on distance from the diagonal), and an offline calibration step with a model-wide allocator chooses the boundaries.

**Result:** Up to 7.88x end-to-end generation speedup over Torch attention and 2.65x over a BF16 baseline, with quality reported as comparable to the original models.

**Critique:** 'Up to' figures against Torch attention inflate the headline; the abstract does not say whether the BF16 baseline is FlashAttention-class, nor compare against SageAttention-style quantized attention or other sparse-attention methods. A purely distance-based policy is static and may miss long-range retrieval heads; needle-style or long-context retrieval results are not given in the abstract. Models, context lengths and GPUs were not readable (full text rate-limited). Code is linked.

### 5. [CompressionBench: How can your coding agents compress LLMs?](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06858)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.06858
_Xinrui Zhong, Zhifeng Li, Zhiming Wang, Yongqi Liu, Shanglin Yang, Yang Zhou et al._

**In one line:** CompressionBench: coding agents get 8 hours to compress 27B-122B models to a storage cap; 29 of 36 submissions pass verification

**What:** A benchmark where a coding agent receives a source LLM, a storage limit, a GPU, a pinned inference runtime and an eight-hour budget, and must deliver compressed weights plus inference code. Six tasks: Nemotron-3.5 120B-A12B and Qwen3.5 122B-A10B at 32/48 GiB, Qwen3.8 27B at 8/12 GiB. Agents see a perplexity-ratio evaluator during search; final acceptance requires fitting the cap and keeping 80% of BF16 mean accuracy on GSM8K, HumanEval and TriviaQA. Six models (Claude Sonnet 5.5, Claude Opus 5.5, GLM 5.3, Kimi K3.1 Pro, MiniMax M3.1 Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) were run through Claude Code.

**Result:** 29/36 submissions pass, including all 18 at the looser limits; at tight limits six fail accuracy and one times out. Best-perplexity artifacts average +3.4% PPL over BF16 and 77.33% accuracy vs 79.56% for BF16. Full-model feedback reversed 17 of 79 earlier local rankings, and compressed models were not uniformly faster (e.g. a dense 12 GiB model at 16.7 tok/s vs 74.6 tok/s for the released GSQ-RCO model).

**Critique:** One run per agent-task pair (four repeats stayed within 0.81% PPL); 50 questions per accuracy benchmark makes the 80% threshold noisy, as the authors note. Decode speed is not scored, so agents can win with slow formats. All agents run inside one harness (Claude Code), which may favour some models. No human-expert or standard-pipeline baseline (e.g. off-the-shelf GPTQ/AWQ at the same cap) is reported in the parts read.

### 6. [Measured Coupling for Parallel Decoding in Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08307)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.08307
_Tal Oved, Lior Wolf_

**In one line:** Diffusion LMs: probes measure how committing one token shifts others' confidence, cutting decode steps at a 1.1-pt avg accuracy cost

**What:** Diffusion LMs must choose which masked positions can be committed together in one step. The standard mean-field rule uses a proxy that penalises positions with similar predictions. This paper instead measures coupling directly—how much committing one token changes the model's confidence at another position—using lightweight probes inside the main forward pass (no training, no extra model), and plugs the measured coupling into the same mean-field selection rule.

**Result:** Across three models and four benchmarks, far fewer decoding steps than mean-field decoding and faster wall-clock in most settings, including with a KV cache, at an average accuracy cost of 1.1 points. Shuffling the measured couplings or replacing them with a uniform bonus removes the speed gain.

**Critique:** The abstract gives no step-count or wall-clock numbers and names only mean-field decoding as a baseline, not other confidence-schedule or learned-parallelism methods. 'Faster in most settings' implies some settings where probe overhead outweighs savings. Models and benchmarks are unnamed in the abstract. No code link.

### 7. [MatMoE: Matryoshka Mixture-of-Experts with Dynamic Mixed-Precision Quantization for Efficient Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01704)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.01704
_Renjie Wei, Haochen Huang, Chuyu Qiu, Jinqi Wen, Dehao Xu, Meng Li_

**In one line:** MatMoE sets each expert's bit-width per decoding step from router scores; 1.3-3.7x over llama.cpp on a Raspberry Pi 5

**What:** Static mixed-precision MoE quantization fixes each expert's bit-width for all decoding steps. MatMoE makes it dynamic: a Matryoshka post-training quantization lets each expert run at several nested precisions within one memory budget, a floor-and-compensation scheme removes redundant memory reads between precision levels, and per-step bit-widths are allocated from the routing scores. Implemented in llama.cpp for CPU and GPU.

**Result:** On three MoE models, 4.0-18.3% higher accuracy than a state-of-the-art static mixed-precision method; 1.3-3.7x speedup over llama.cpp on Raspberry Pi 5 and 1.2-1.3x on RTX 4090 and Jetson AGX Orin.

**Critique:** The abstract does not name the MoE models, the static baseline, or the bit-widths behind the llama.cpp comparison, so the speedups could partly reflect lower average precision rather than the method. Accuracy gains of up to 18.3% suggest the static baseline is at very low bits. Gains on the RTX 4090 are modest. No code link.

### 8. [BitLLM: Efficient Low-Bit, Conversion-Hiding LLM Decoding on Mobile GPUs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00547)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.00547
_Hangyu Zheng, Zhihao Shu, Kunxiong Zhu, Minghai Qin, Xiaolong Ma, Gagan Agrawal et al._

**In one line:** BitLLM hides quantize/dequantize conversions on mobile GPUs: 1.47-1.78x avg decode speedup over llama.cpp, MNN-LLM, LiteRT-LM, MLC

**What:** Low-bit quantization underperforms on phone GPUs because of frequent conversions between quantized and floating-point values and irregular KV-cache memory access as context grows. BitLLM is an inference engine with a new KV-cache layout and packing scheme and an integer-centric pipeline that moves type conversions off the critical computation path.

**Result:** Average 1.47-1.78x speedup over llama.cpp, MNN-LLM, LiteRT-LM and MLC-LLM on off-the-shelf mobile GPUs, and up to 88.9% lower per-token energy in long-context generation.

**Critique:** Devices, models, bit-widths and context lengths are not given in the abstract; 'up to 88.9%' energy is a best case. Comparing engines at possibly different quantization formats can confound kernel efficiency with format choice; accuracy at the chosen bit-widths is not reported in the abstract. No code link.

NVIDIA blog · news · inference

**In one line:** NVIDIA: Vera Rubin NVL72 up to 30x agentic throughput per dollar vs GB300 on SemiAnalysis AgentX, with ~3% more HBM per GPU

**What:** NVIDIA's 5 Nov post argues that memory efficiency, not HBM capacity, drives agentic inference cost. Techniques listed: NVFP4 weights and KV cache (vs FP8), sharding weights across all 72 GPUs, Vera CPU LPDDR over NVLink-C2C as a holding tier for paused context, Dynamo moving inactive KV cache to a shared CMX storage tier and back, and keeping RAG indexes on CPU/storage via cuVS. **Critique:** Vendor claims with 'up to' maxima on a single benchmark/model; the two comparisons use different per-user speed targets. No methodology for cost per dollar, batch size, context length or precision settings, and no quantified effect for NVFP4 KV, CPU-memory offload or CMX tiering. Not independently verified; Rubin systems are not yet broadly available for third-party benchmarking.
