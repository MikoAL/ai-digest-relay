### 1. [Folding Language Models into Themselves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18113)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.18113
_Ziyang Zhang, Yubin Jing, Yuyao Li, Haofan Wang, Yichen Gong_

**In one line:** Concertina folds groups of adjacent Qwen3-4B layers into one looped shared block; 2.27B student keeps ~84% of teacher avg

**What:** Builds a compact student directly from teacher weights instead of training from scratch. Every group of N adjacent teacher layers is folded into one shared block executed N times (a looped/recurrent-depth transformer); each loop step adds a low-rank per-step correction, and a per-token gate mixes these corrections so the shared block can approximate the original layers. The folded model is then distilled with a mix of general, math, academic and multiple-choice data. Code is linked from the abstract page (github.com/myboker/concertina).

**Result:** From Qwen3-4B, a 2.27B Concertina model distilled on 22B tokens retains about 90% of the teacher on commonsense and math reasoning and 84% on average across eight benchmarks; the authors report it beats same-size dense and layer-sharing baselines and Qwen3-1.7B (trained on >1000x more data).

**Critique:** Only one teacher (Qwen3-4B) and one student size are reported in the abstract. Looping saves parameters but not FLOPs per token: the shared block still runs N times, so latency/throughput versus a dense 2.27B model is the key missing number. Comparison to Qwen3-1.7B is apples-to-oranges (different size, no teacher). Which layer-sharing baselines were used and whether they got the same 22B distillation tokens is not stated in the abstract.

### 2. [Fuse-All MoE Inference Kernel with Load Balancing on TPUs](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.17857)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.17857
_Rupeng Liu, Bangsheng Tang, Zongwei Zhou, Chengji Yao, Yejin Lee_

**In one line:** Fused TPU MoE kernel keeps intermediates on chip, rebalances EP16 prefill; up to 34.5% serving throughput over Megablox EP16

**What:** A single TPU kernel for MoE inference that fuses token collection, expert up/gate/down projections, gated activation and output writes, keeping intermediates on chip. Fixed token-slot destinations let scheduling and expert placement change without changing routing. In prefill-decode disaggregated serving, EP16 prefill is balanced by scheduling plus expert redistribution; for decode, a symmetric EP8TP2 layout splits active-expert compute across the two TensorCores of each chip.

**Result:** On a skewed EP16 prefill workload, kernel time drops from 2.3x to 1.09x the balanced-workload reference. EP8TP2 cuts decode kernel latency by up to 21.3% versus fused EP16 at 32-512 decode tokens. With serving-stack optimizations, system throughput improves by up to 34.5% over the Megablox EP16 baseline.

**Critique:** Numbers are 'up to' figures; the abstract does not name the MoE models, TPU generation or traffic traces, and the 34.5% end-to-end gain bundles the kernel with unspecified serving-stack optimizations, so the kernel's own share is unclear. No GPU comparison (e.g. DeepEP/grouped-GEMM) and TPU-only applicability. Details from the listing abstract only.

### 3. [Delayed Effects of Temporary Weight Decay in Muon Pretraining](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.17876)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.17876
_Md Rifat Arefin, Ravid Shwartz-Ziv, Ernie Chang, Chinnadhurai Sankar, Rylan Conway, Aristide Baratin et al._

**In one line:** Temporary weight decay in Muon pretraining can hurt loss when it ends yet win by the final budget, from 162M to 3.4B models

**What:** Studies weight-decay schedules where decay is applied only in a window, for decoder-only Transformers trained with Muon at 162M, 405M, 1.4B and 3.4B parameters. Moving a fixed-length window from early to late training reverses its final effect (up to 1.4B), and window duration matters even at matched total shrinkage. Norm analysis: early decay leaves a lasting weight-norm deficit that keeps relative update sizes larger after the window; norm-reset experiments at 162M/405M attribute most of the final gain to the end-of-window norm.

**Result:** A schedule tuned at 162M and transferred unchanged to 1.4B is 0.26 nats worse than no decay right after the window ends, overtakes no-decay after 73% of training, and finishes 0.028 nats better, also beating the best sampled constant-decay baseline.

**Critique:** The final gain (0.028 nats) is small and no seed variance is given in the abstract. The constant-decay baseline is the best of a sample, not a full sweep. Mechanism experiments (norm reset) are only at 162M/405M, and the window-position reversal is stated only up to 1.4B, so the 3.4B role is unclear. Downstream-task effects are not reported. The practical lesson (judge schedules at the final budget, not mid-run) matters for small-proxy hyperparameter transfer.

### 4. [TIDE: Reclaiming Lost Performance In On-Policy Reinforcement Learning](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.17924)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.17924
_Mathew Jacob, Morgan Borjigin-Wang, Kan Zhu, Po Hao Chen, Yi Pan, Andy Ruan et al._

**In one line:** TIDE repurposes idle rollout GPUs for training and migrates long-tail rollouts; up to 1.36x end-to-end over colocated RL

**What:** In synchronous, colocated on-policy RL post-training of LLMs, long-tail rollouts leave inference engines idle or running at tiny batch sizes. TIDE keeps strict on-policy semantics but (1) repurposes rollout GPUs for training while the last rollouts finish and (2) migrates in-flight rollouts between inference engines to consolidate them and raise decode throughput. An adaptive policy decides when to repurpose resources without per-workload tuning.

**Result:** Up to 1.36x end-to-end speedup over colocated systems, outperforming the on-policy baselines evaluated, and up to 97% of optimal throughput.

**Critique:** 'Up to' numbers; the abstract does not name the models, cluster size, RL algorithm or the specific baseline frameworks. Comparison is against on-policy systems only, not asynchronous/off-policy pipelines that attack the same long-tail problem by relaxing semantics. How 'optimal throughput' is defined is not stated in the abstract.

### 5. [User-Schedulable Approximations for Quantized Tensor Programs](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.17526)

arXiv · paper · efficiency · arXiv:2611.17526
_Alex Reinking_

**In one line:** Halide extension generates 347 kernels for 31 GGML/KleidiAI quant formats with 3.7x less code; median 1.13x on M3 Max

**What:** Extends Halide's algorithm/schedule separation to value representations: each quantization format is an encoder-decoder 'codec' composed from reusable quantizer, integer, bit-packing and layout units, and a new approximate_by directive lets the scheduler place both decoders inside matrix-product kernels. Optional round-trip property tests found an inconsistent block sum in GGML's q8_1 format.

**Result:** One algorithm plus one schedule library generates 347 kernels for 31 GGML and KleidiAI formats on Arm CPUs and Apple GPUs, covering 38 weight-activation pairs GGML lacks, with 3.7x less code. On Apple M3 Max, kernels reach at least 95% of GGML's format-specific kernel speed in 91.0% of cases that have one, median speedup 1.13x. An x86/Intel AMX port matches GGML in 83.5% of its cases.

**Critique:** Kernel-level microbenchmarks; no end-to-end tokens/s for a model in llama.cpp is reported in the abstract. Coverage is Arm CPU, Apple GPU and x86 AMX, not NVIDIA/AMD GPUs. ~9% of M3 Max cases and ~16% of AMX cases fall below parity. Single-author work; the q8_1 inconsistency claim would need confirmation by GGML maintainers.

### 7. [The State Is Not the Bottleneck: A Paged-State Gated-DeltaNet Tile on a Tri-Mode Multicast Mesh, Bit-Exact from RTL to Silicon](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.18422)

arXiv · paper · architecture · arXiv:2611.18422
_Cong Xu, Ravi Sankar_

**In one line:** FPGA tile runs a hybrid Gated DeltaNet layer with recurrent state paged off-chip; paging is at most 11.2% of per-token traffic

**What:** Hardware tile computing one layer of a custom 369M-parameter hybrid Gated DeltaNet (linear-attention/delta-rule recurrent) model on an Agilex 5 FPGA. The 6.2 MiB recurrent state does not fit in 0.87 MiB of block RAM, so it is paged to external memory. The tile is one node of a multicast mesh of tri-mode processing elements; hardware matches a fixed-point reference bit for bit.

**Result:** Paging adds 26.0 MB of memory traffic per decoded token, at most 11.2% of total; one token takes 11.15 ms for this layer at 75 MHz, with weight matrix-vector products ~62% and the state phase (incl. paging) ~13% of time. State logic matches the reference on all 18 recurrent layers in simulation; six builds passed all 30 runs of 64 tokens and two runs of 1,024 tokens on board.

**Critique:** Single layer, synthetic weights and a custom 369M model, so no model-quality or end-to-end throughput figures. No baseline (CPU/GPU/other FPGA designs, or a softmax-attention KV-cache comparison) is named. Long-sequence testing is minimal (two 1,024-token runs). Useful mainly as evidence that linear-attention state, not weights, is cheap to page at the edge.

### 8. [Callo: Serving Cache-Miss Requests with Speculative Decoding on Invalidated KV under Prefill-Decode Disaggregation](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.18459)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.18459
_Jianjun Ou, Matthew Lentz_

**In one line:** Callo drafts tokens from stale KV after agent context edits, verifies after re-prefill; 1.05-1.19x over vLLM at moderate load

**What:** Agent sessions edit context often, invalidating cached KV and forcing re-prefill before decode. In a prefill-decode disaggregated system, Callo uses the invalidated KV to speculatively draft tokens while prefill runs, then verifies drafts against the exact KV before committing. Speculation is scheduled into decode-underutilized periods and preempted when acceptance looks unlikely.

**Result:** On a two-GPU testbed with SWE-agent sessions across three model families: all-request speedups of 1.05-1.19x over vLLM at moderate load; trace-driven cluster simulation suggests the gains hold at scale.

**Critique:** Modest gains on a two-GPU testbed; scale claims rest on simulation. Only moderate load is reported in the abstract; behaviour at high load (where speculation competes for decode capacity) is unclear. The three model families are not named in the abstract. Baseline is vLLM, not a prefix-cache-aware or other speculative system.

### 9. [Superfluid: Local AI Server for Distributed LLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17753)

arXiv · paper · inference · arXiv:2611.17753
_Prabod Rathnayaka, Isura Manchanayake, Srikandabala Kogul, Steve Siu, Fabian Waschkowski, Lukas Wesemann_

**In one line:** Superfluid local LLM server: 1.5 s first token vs 291-612 s for llama-server/Ollama for a chat on an Apple M5 Pro

**What:** Open-source local LLM server that runs llama.cpp, MLX or BaseRT backends in isolated workers under one scheduler. The scheduler prioritizes interactive requests over background ones, computes a prompt prefix shared by many requests once, and can span multiple machines as a single endpoint.

**Result:** Serving Qwen3.8-27B on an Apple M5 Pro, a chat request gets its first token in 1.5 s versus 291-612 s on llama-server, Ollama and mlx_lm.server (per the abstract's scenario). Across six machines, throughput grows from 112 to 534 tok/s and median time-to-first-token falls from 99 to 12 s.

**Critique:** The 291-612 s baselines reflect a specific contention scenario (interactive request queued behind background work) that the baselines are not designed to prioritize, so the ratio overstates general speedups. The listing abstract was truncated, so workload details are incomplete. Six-machine scaling is 4.8x, sublinear, and hardware mix is not stated. Systems integration rather than a new algorithm.

### 10. [LACCO: Local Accumulation with Computation-Communication Overlap](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2611.17991)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.17991
_Daniele Kopyshevskiy, Benjamin Thérien, Nicolas Pinto, Edouard Oyallon_

**In one line:** LACCO overlaps DiLoCo-style global sync with local compute without stale outer updates; beats Eager/Delayed DiLoCo to 0.5B

**What:** Two-stage method for training LMs over low-bandwidth links. Like overlapped DiLoCo variants it hides global communication behind local computation, but it still applies the globally aggregated update to every replica, so the outer update is not stale (unlike Eager and Delayed DiLoCo). Convergence is proved for smooth non-convex objectives.

**Result:** On FineWeb LM pretraining, LACCO outperforms Eager and Delayed DiLoCo, with gains holding up to 0.5B parameters, across several communication intervals and up to 16 workers, with negligible or fully overlappable compute and memory overhead. The abstract gives no loss or speed numbers.

**Critique:** No quantitative gains in the abstract. Small scale (≤0.5B, ≤16 workers) for a method aimed at large decentralized training. No wall-clock comparison under real low-bandwidth links is mentioned, and the non-overlapped DiLoCo baseline is not mentioned.

### 11. [See Every Block Once: Variance-Reduced Post-Training for Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17754)

arXiv · paper · training · arXiv:2611.17754
_Yunzhen He, Hiroaki Yamagiwa, Hidetoshi Shimodaira_

**In one line:** Full-block ELBO estimator for masked diffusion LM post-training beats random block sampling by 12.79 points on average

**What:** Post-training for block-wise masked diffusion LMs usually estimates the blockwise ELBO surrogate by sampling a random block index. This paper evaluates every block exactly once per sequence, claiming an unbiased estimator with lower variance.

**Result:** Across five math, planning and coding benchmarks, outperforms the random-block-sampling baseline by an average of 12.79 points and achieves the highest accuracy on four benchmarks.

**Critique:** Evaluating every block raises per-step compute, and whether the comparison is compute-matched is not stated in the abstract. The base diffusion model and other post-training baselines (e.g. other variance-reduction methods) are not named. A 12.79-point average gain from an estimator change is large and needs independent replication.

### 12. [More Agents, More Samples, or More Thinking? Untangling Coordination, Ensembling, and Test-Time Compute in Multi-Agent LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17610)

arXiv · paper · other · arXiv:2611.17610
_Seyed Mahmoud Sajjadi Mohammadabadi_

**In one line:** Meta-analysis of 516 multi-agent LLM configs: vs compute-matched baselines, multi-agent averages -0.042 accuracy

**What:** Meta-analysis of nine studies covering 516 multi-agent configurations, separating coordination effects from sample count and test-time compute with a resource vector and baseline categories.

**Result:** Against baselines matched on sample count or resource budget, multi-agent systems average -0.042 accuracy, and equal or fall below the baseline in 74% of comparable contrasts.
